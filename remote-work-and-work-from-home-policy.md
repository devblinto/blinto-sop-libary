# Remote Work & Work-from-Home Policy

## Purpose

Explain when Blinto may approve temporary work from home (WFH) and the expectations that apply on an approved remote-working day.

**Blinto is an office-first company. It does not promote or encourage routine work from home.** WFH is an exception to help maintain safe and practical operations during genuine emergency or other justified situations; it is not a normal alternative working arrangement or an automatic employee benefit.

## Scope

This policy covers requests from **confirmed employees** whose responsibilities can be performed effectively away from the office. Requests are reviewed individually.

This policy does not establish a regular hybrid schedule, a general right to work from home, or a standing entitlement to remote days. Any different arrangement required by law, an applicable agreement, or an approved accommodation must be considered separately by HR.

## When WFH May Be Considered

Examples include:

- serious weather, flooding, or transport disruption affecting safe travel;
- a temporary safety or other urgent situation that makes office attendance impractical; or
- an occasional exceptional circumstance that HR considers justified.

An emergency does not automatically mean an employee is fit or able to work. An employee who is unwell and unable to perform duties should discuss appropriate leave or another lawful arrangement rather than being expected to work while sick.

Approval depends on the reason, whether the duties can be completed remotely, expected availability, business and client needs, and any applicable legal or contractual requirements.

## Request and Approval

**Department Head recommendation → HR final approval.**

For a foreseeable request, the employee should apply **at least one working day in advance** through HRMS, where the relevant request function is available. The employee should explain the reason, requested date or period, and ability to perform normal duties.

The Department Head considers task suitability, necessary meetings, and client/team coverage, then recommends approval or refusal. **Only HR gives final approval.** An employee should not treat a request, manager recommendation, or informal conversation as approval.

For a genuine same-day emergency, employees should inform the Department Head and HR **as soon as reasonably possible**. HR may approve an emergency exception. Where advance contact is genuinely impossible, the employee must report the situation as soon as reasonably possible; HR will then review the appropriate attendance or leave treatment.

Any recurring or extended exception requires advance written HR approval. Such approval remains exceptional and does not establish a standard hybrid arrangement.

HR communicates the decision and applicable period; rejected requests should not be recorded as approved WFH.

## Working Hours and Communication

An approved WFH day is a **normal working day**, not a day of leave.

Unless HR approves a different schedule in writing:

- normal office hours remain **8:30 AM–5:00 PM**;
- employees join the **9:00 AM daily stand-up** remotely;
- the normal lunch and break arrangements under the Office Hours Policy continue;
- employees remain reasonably reachable through approved work channels and attend required online meetings; and
- employees carry out their assigned duties, communicate blockers, and maintain work continuity.

Working beyond normal hours must follow the **Overtime Policy**, including its approval and legal requirements. Remote work approval does not by itself authorize overtime.

## Attendance and Work Progress

On an approved remote day, employees should:

- check in and out using **HRMS** where its relevant attendance functionality is available;
- record and update work in **ClickUp** or the approved departmental task-management system;
- remain reasonably available and perform assigned work; and
- inform the Department Head promptly about any material delay or interruption.

The **Department Head verifies the remote-work attendance and relevant work activity**. HR maintains or corrects the official attendance record under the Attendance Policy.

An approved remote day must be recorded as an approved work arrangement rather than an unexplained absence. Employees may ask HR to review an inaccurate record. Employees must not falsify check-ins, work logs, or availability.

WFH cannot be used in place of approved leave when the employee is not actually available to work.

## Internet, Power, and Other Disruptions

Employees should promptly tell the Department Head if internet, electricity, equipment, or other problems prevent reliable work, and make reasonable efforts to restore service when safe and practical.

The Department Head may adjust assignments or reschedule work. HR reviews whether the time can properly remain recorded as worked time, requires an attendance correction, or should be addressed through leave or another lawful arrangement. A technical interruption does not automatically justify an unauthorized wage deduction or disciplinary penalty.

An employee should not expose themselves to unsafe conditions merely to remain online during an emergency.

## Equipment, Workspace, and Expenses

Employees normally use their **own internet connection** and pay ordinary household electricity expenses. An approved company laptop may be used where available and authorized.

Company equipment remains Blinto property and must be protected from theft, damage, and access by unauthorized persons. Employees should work from a reasonably safe and suitable environment.

**WFH does not carry an automatic allowance.** Special equipment, data expenses, or reimbursements require separate approval. Employees should not assume that incurring an expense creates a right to reimbursement.

## Information Security and Confidentiality

Employees must:

- use approved company accounts, devices, and tools;
- follow required multi-factor authentication and password protections;
- never share company accounts, devices, or credentials with family members or other unauthorized people;
- protect company and client information from being seen, overheard, copied, or accessed by others;
- avoid public or shared computers for sensitive company work;
- use an appropriately secure internet connection; and
- report lost devices, suspected account compromise, or possible data exposure promptly using the applicable security/escalation process.

The **Credential & Password Management Policy** and **Access Provisioning & Deprovisioning** SOP own the detailed security and access rules; this policy does not replace them.

## Safety, Leave, and Employee Rights

Employees should choose a reasonably safe place to work and follow applicable emergency advice. If circumstances make work unsafe or impossible, they should inform HR and the Department Head as soon as practical.

Approved annual, casual, sick, or other leave remains governed by the **Leave & Time-Off Policy**. A request for emergency WFH must not be used to sidestep leave rules, nor must this policy override legally protected leave, working-time limits, overtime entitlement, or other statutory rights.

An employee who requests a reasonable emergency exception may receive a decision based on operational suitability, subject to applicable law. The company will review any special statutory accommodation or contractual obligation separately.

## Responsibilities

**Employee:** Explain the need honestly, request approval, attend required meetings, maintain work updates, protect information, and promptly report disruptions.

**Department Head:** Assess whether remote work is feasible, recommend the request to HR, coordinate work and coverage, and verify the applicable attendance/work record.

**People Operations / HR:** Give final approval or refusal, handle urgent exceptions, communicate approved dates, and maintain accurate employment and attendance records.

## Questions and Disputes

Employees may contact HR to clarify eligibility, request an emergency exception, or challenge a WFH attendance decision. HR should consider the employee's explanation, the Department Head's input, official records, and applicable law before confirming the outcome.
