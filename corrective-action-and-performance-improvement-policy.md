# Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy

> **Management-approved v1.3 (12 November 2026).** This version supersedes the previous v1.2. For involuntary termination as a possible final corrective action, the internal approval route is **Department Lead recommendation → HR review → CEO final approval**, consistent with the approved [Employee Offboarding](./employee-offboarding.md). Applicable law and stronger contractual rights always prevail.

## Purpose

Help employees understand performance and conduct expectations, receive timely feedback, and have a fair opportunity to improve. Establish consistent, confidential procedures for coaching, warnings, performance improvement plans (PIPs), investigations, and escalation.

The purpose is to correct problems whenever reasonably possible—not to create a fixed punishment ladder or use a PIP as a substitute for statutory disciplinary procedures.

## Scope

This policy applies company-wide, subject to the individual's actual employment classification, contract, applicable Bangladesh law, and any stronger protections. It addresses:

- repeated lateness, attendance failures, and unauthorized absence;
- repeated low KPI performance, missed deadlines, poor-quality work, and failure to take reasonable ownership;
- failure to follow lawful, communicated company SOPs and reasonable instructions;
- inappropriate workplace conduct and repeated communication failures; and
- alleged dishonesty, fraud, harassment, security violations, or other potentially serious misconduct requiring a separate investigation.

An employee's approved or legally protected leave, a genuine emergency, an approved accommodation, disputed attendance data, or good-faith exercise of rights must not automatically be treated as misconduct. An alleged breach is not a finding of guilt.

## Core Principles

1. **Fairness and improvement:** explain the issue, provide relevant support, and permit a meaningful response.
2. **Evidence over assumption:** use dates, work products, ClickUp tasks, KPI definitions, attendance records, communications, and other verifiable facts; check for data errors.
3. **Proportionate action:** consider seriousness, recurrence, operational impact, prior support, role expectations, and reasonable mitigating circumstances.
4. **Separate pathways:** distinguish routine coaching, capability/performance issues, unauthorized-absence administration, and formal allegations of misconduct.
5. **No automatic punishment:** warning counts, HRMS flags, rejected leave, and PIP results do not independently authorize dismissal or unpaid deductions.
6. **Due process:** no company response deadline may shorten the minimum response/hearing/inquiry rights required by law.
7. **Confidentiality and consistency:** share records only with people who need them and apply comparable standards fairly.
8. **No retaliation:** an employee may explain facts, correct records, raise protected concerns, or participate in an inquiry without retaliation.

## The Five-Stage Corrective Framework

These five stages provide Blinto's normal sequence for ordinary performance, attendance, and conduct concerns. They are a management framework, **not a substitute for statutory misconduct proceedings**. HR may close, repeat, or adjust an internal stage where the circumstances warrant; a serious allegation may be referred immediately for a legally compliant investigation rather than proceeding step by step. No stage or warning count automatically authorizes termination.

### Stage 1 — Coaching / Verbal Reminder

The Department Head or responsible manager:

1. describes the concern using specific examples;
2. clarifies the expected standard, KPI, task deadline, or relevant policy;
3. asks about obstacles, training, workload, tools, and relevant circumstances;
4. agrees on a practical improvement action and follow-up; and
5. retains brief, factual coaching notes in an appropriately restricted location.

This is ordinarily informal, not a disciplinary finding. The employee should know when a conversation is only coaching.

### Stage 2 — Show-Cause Letter and Employee Explanation

If a problem continues after coaching, or is sufficiently material to require a written explanation, the Department Head refers the facts and supporting records to HR. **HR issues the show-cause letter before any Stage 3 documented warning is decided.** Issuing the letter requests an explanation; it is **not itself a warning or a finding of misconduct**.

The show-cause letter must clearly state:

- the specific concern or alleged conduct, dates, applicable standards, and evidence available for review;
- what response or supporting documentation the employee may submit;
- the delivery/receipt date and the applicable response deadline;
- how to send the response to HR and request a meeting or reasonable extension; and
- that HR will consider the employee's explanation and verified evidence before deciding whether any action is warranted.

**Ordinary non-statutory cases:** The employee must normally respond **within three (3) working days after receiving the show-cause letter**. Count the three working days from the next scheduled working day after confirmed receipt; do not count weekly holidays or declared company holidays. HR may allow a reasonable extension for illness, emergency, inaccessible records, or other justified circumstances. HR records the proof of receipt, explanation, evidence, and any extension. If there is no response by the applicable deadline, HR may decide the ordinary internal matter on the verified available evidence, without presuming guilt.

**Mandatory statutory exception:** Where the allegation and contemplated punishment engage **sections 23–24 of the Bangladesh Labour Act**, the employee must be given **at least seven days to explain** and all other applicable statutory safeguards, including an opportunity to be heard and the prescribed inquiry process. The three-working-day internal deadline **does not apply** and cannot shorten those legal rights. HR should use a statutory-compliant charge/show-cause process rather than relabeling a disciplinary matter as an ordinary one.

After reviewing the response, HR may close the case without warning, provide further coaching, correct a mistaken record, proceed to an appropriate next stage, or refer the matter for a formal statutory inquiry. HR communicates the result and reasons in writing.

### Stage 3 — Documented Warning Letter

Where the Stage 2 review establishes a recurring or material concern that warrants a written management response, **HR issues the documented warning letter**. The Department Head recommends the action and provides evidence, but does not independently issue the warning.

The letter must identify the verified concern, employee's explanation and relevant mitigating circumstances, expected correction, support to be provided, review period, and possible next steps if the issue continues. HR records the decision, issues the letter through the approved restricted channel, and provides the employee with a copy. Employee acknowledgement confirms receipt, **not agreement**.

A documented warning must not be issued merely because a show-cause letter was sent or the response was disputed. If the proposed warning amounts to statutory disciplinary punishment, **complete the legally required procedure before issuance**.

### Stage 4 — Formal Warning Letter or Performance Improvement Plan (PIP)

If material concerns persist after appropriate earlier intervention, or a significant issue justifies escalation, HR considers **a formal warning letter or a structured PIP** based on the nature of the problem, with no automatic requirement to impose both.

**Formal warning letter:** HR approves and issues a formal written warning based on verified facts and the employee's opportunity to respond, subject to the applicable statutory disciplinary process. The letter specifies the concern, the improvement required, the timeframe for review, support available, and potential consequences of continued non-improvement.

**PIP:** Intended primarily for measurable capability, work-quality, deadlines, or KPI gaps capable of improvement through guidance and reasonable support—not as a substitute for investigating alleged serious misconduct. The standard period is **30 calendar days**, unless HR documents an appropriate alternative. It must define:

- specific gaps and role/KPI expectations supported by evidence;
- measurable improvement goals, milestones, and assessment method;
- reasonable tools, coaching, training, or other support;
- start and end dates, check-in responsibilities, and written progress feedback;
- the employee's comments and relevant circumstances; and
- possible outcomes, without presenting termination as automatic.

The Department Head proposes the goals, monitors progress, and provides feedback. HR verifies fairness, approves the formal warning or PIP, maintains the record, and communicates the outcome. The dedicated **Performance Improvement Plan Procedure** and Appendix A below apply.

### Stage 5 — Further Formal Action: Final Warning and Possible Termination

If significant concerns continue after the appropriate preceding action, HR conducts a documented review of the facts, improvement history, employee's explanation, legal classification, and applicable alternatives. Depending on seriousness and fairness, **a final warning letter may be issued** under the required approval and legal procedures. The final warning must specify what remains unresolved, the changes expected, any final review date, and that continued failure may lead to further lawful employment action.

If sufficient improvement still does not occur, or a legally established serious ground warrants consideration, **termination may be considered as a possible final action—not an automatic consequence**. Before any termination, HR must verify grounds, applicable investigation and disciplinary safeguards, notice or other legal requirements, compensation, contract terms, and final settlement; the employee must receive all legally required opportunities to respond.

**Final corrective-action approval flow for involuntary termination:** **Department Lead recommendation → HR review → CEO final approval**. The Department Lead submits the documented recommendation and supporting evidence; HR independently reviews the facts, the employee’s response, the applicable statutory process, contract terms, notice and settlement obligations, and available alternatives; the CEO makes the **final internal approval decision**. HR communicates and implements a termination decision only after CEO approval and completion of legally required procedures. This is consistent with [Employee Offboarding](./employee-offboarding.md). A final warning remains within HR’s warning-approval authority; a final warning does not itself authorize termination. Any statutory authorization/signature requirements also apply.

HR may close the matter, arrange coaching, propose justified reassignment, or approve an appropriate PIP extension when supported by evidence and lawful terms. Serious misconduct may be handled immediately under the separate statutory-investigation procedure without treating the five stages as mandatory successive punishments.

## Attendance, Unauthorized Absence & Leave

Apply [Attendance Policy](./attendance-policy.md) for late-arrival administration and attendance correction. The approved main-branch Attendance Policy currently contains an HRMS late-arrival escalation; a separate attendance v1.5 draft is pending review. Do not imply that a draft automation rule has already replaced the approved version.

For an absence after a leave request was rejected or when no leave was approved:

1. HRMS may flag the date **Pending Review / Potentially Unauthorized**, but the flag is not itself a disciplinary verdict.
2. HR checks punches, HRMS leave requests, available balances, communications, emergency circumstances, legal entitlements, and record-correction requests.
3. The employee receives the allegation or discrepancy and a reasonable chance to explain and correct evidence. For ordinary record clarification, use the proposed three-working-day window with reasonable extensions. Use the longer statutory process when a disciplinary punishment is contemplated.
4. HR records a reasoned attendance/leave classification and communicates the decision. If the absence is not paid under law, contract, or valid leave rules, Finance may administer a **lawful, correctly calculated** unpaid absence; do not use arbitrary salary penalties.
5. Any disciplinary sanction for alleged misconduct follows the applicable statutory procedure, not merely an HRMS status or unfavorable explanation.

An available leave balance does not automatically authorize absence after a legitimate rejection, but rejection also cannot extinguish an applicable statutory or protected leave right. HR must distinguish a scheduling disagreement from protected sickness or emergency absence.

## Serious Misconduct and Statutory Disciplinary Procedure

Alleged theft, fraud, document tampering, harassment, threats, violence, major security breaches, and other serious allegations must be referred immediately to HR. HR should preserve relevant evidence, prevent retaliation, and arrange a fair, impartial investigation. The Chief of Staff reviews serious or disputed cases as appropriate, subject to independence and confidentiality.

For workers and measures governed by **sections 23–24 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, as amended**, the company must apply the actual current statutory procedure rather than the ordinary three-working-day explanation workflow. In particular, section 24 requires written allegations and a copy to the worker, **at least seven days to explain**, an opportunity to be heard, the required investigation/inquiry arrangements, and properly authorized disciplinary action. The current statutory provisions also regulate matters such as enquiry composition, timeframe, assistance, cross-examination, and suspension. HR must verify and implement the applicable consolidated 2026 text and Labour Rules before commencing a case.

Temporary restriction of access may be used to contain a genuine security or safety risk; it is not itself a finding of guilt or a substitute for the lawful rules governing suspension, pay, or discipline. A statutory suspension must be documented and handled under applicable law.

HR should seek qualified legal review for potentially dismissible misconduct, contested evidence, or uncertainty about worker classification or due process. Blinto must not characterize a misconduct dismissal as an ordinary performance or contractual termination merely to bypass statutory requirements.

## Performance Improvement Plan Procedure

### 1. Open the PIP

The Department Head proposes a PIP to HR, explaining measurable performance gaps and relevant prior feedback. HR checks role expectations, evidence quality, potential mitigating factors, and whether a PIP is appropriate.

### 2. Discuss and issue the plan

HR and the Department Head meet the employee, explain the evidence and targets, invite their explanation, and record any disagreement or reasonable support need. HR issues a copy of the approved plan, normally for 30 calendar days.

### 3. Monitor progress

The Department Head provides practical guidance and objective written progress feedback, normally at **Day 10 and Day 20**, with a final review near **Day 30**. These proposed milestones are guidance rather than a separate formal performance-review program. HR monitors whether the manager delivered promised support.

### 4. Conduct the final review

Assess each goal against agreed measures, consider work conditions and approved absences, give the employee a chance to comment, and document one of the following:

- **Met:** PIP closes; routine development and performance review continue.
- **Partially met / reasonable improvement:** a justified, documented extension or additional coaching may be considered.
- **Not met:** HR may consider another proportionate, lawful management step; no automatic warning, dismissal, or other penalty follows merely from the PIP score.
- **Role-fit issue:** consider reasonable reassignment or clarified responsibilities if feasible, subject to lawful employment terms.

A PIP extension must specify what remains unmet, why extra time is justified, the support to be provided, and a new review date. The employee receives the outcome in writing and may request HR review of factual inaccuracies.

## Warning Status, Review & Closure

The company proposes that a documented, formal, or final warning have an **active management-review period of six months from issuance** for ordinary recurrence assessment, subject to final management approval. This is **not** a statutory expiry, does not erase evidence, and does not compel an escalated sanction. HR may close the active action sooner or later based on documented sustained improvement and circumstances.

**Decision pending:** management must expressly approve or modify the suggested six-month active period before it becomes an operative HRMS rule. Until then, use a documented case-specific review/closure date rather than applying an unapproved automatic period.

Closed warnings and PIP records may be retained under lawful retention and privacy controls and considered later only where relevant and fair. There is no automatic dismissal based on a number of warnings.

## Roles & Approval

| Role | Responsibility | Authority |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Employee | Responds to concerns, participates in improvement, corrects records, and may request review | Has response/hearing rights; no duty to concede disputed allegations |
| Department Head | Gives coaching, documents evidence, proposes targets and referrals, monitors improvement | Informal coaching and recommendation; **does not independently issue formal warnings or terminate** |
| People Operations / HR | Administers cases, reviews law/records, issues show-cause letters and documented/formal/final warnings, monitors PIPs and retains confidential documentation | **Final internal authority for show-cause and documented/formal/final warnings**, subject to statutory disciplinary safeguards; **reviews and recommends on termination but does not give final termination approval** |
| Chief of Staff | Reviews serious, disputed, high-impact, or sensitive recommendations | Advisory/management review, not a substitute for statutory inquiry independence |
| CEO | Reviews involuntary termination under currently approved Employee Offboarding SOP | **Final internal approval for involuntary termination**, following Department Lead recommendation and HR review |
| Finance | Implements only lawful, authorized payroll changes and final settlement | Payroll calculation and controlled processing; no disciplinary decision-making |

## Records, HRMS & Confidentiality

Blinto's target workflow is a dedicated restricted **HRMS corrective-action module**, with a notification and audit trail. Until that module is confirmed available and tested, HR may maintain a controlled confidential case folder in Google Drive. Do **not** assume the proposed HRMS module exists or currently supports every field.

The case record should include: employee ID and department; relevant dates; case category; specific allegations or performance gaps; evidence links; applicable HRMS attendance/leave corrections; coaching history; show-cause delivery/receipt proof, applicable response deadline and any extension; employee explanation; witnesses/inquiry documents where lawful and necessary; review and approval history; PIP goals, support and check-ins; written outcome; acknowledgement; review or closure date; and any later correction.

Department Heads may maintain limited factual coaching notes but should not keep sensitive investigation material in ordinary ClickUp tasks or broadly shared team drives. HR controls access to formal case files. Employees should receive copies of their own formal warnings, responses, and applicable decisions, subject to lawful confidentiality and privacy limitations. Acknowledgement means receipt, not agreement. Define retention/deletion periods with legal and organizational approval rather than inventing a fixed retention period.

## Employee Response, Review & Escalation

An employee may ask HR to correct an inaccurate record, provide supporting material, explain circumstances, or request reconsideration. HR must record the issue and provide a reasoned response. Serious or conflicted matters go to an independent appropriate reviewer or leadership, and statutory remedies remain available.

Escalate immediately where an allegation involves possible discrimination, retaliation, harassment, workplace safety, fraud, crime, serious security issues, a conflict of interest, potentially unlawful wage deductions, proposed suspension or termination, or disputed statutory rights.
