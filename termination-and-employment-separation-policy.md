# Termination & Employment Separation Policy

## Purpose

Explain the different ways an employment relationship may end, how Blinto makes and communicates employer-initiated separation decisions, and how employees' notice, due process, and financial rights are protected.

**Not every separation is the same kind of termination.** A performance concern, proven misconduct, an eliminated position, and medically certified incapacity have different legal meanings and may involve different procedures and payments.

## Scope and Legal Classification

This policy covers employer-initiated separation of Blinto employees, including applicable probation outcomes. The correct procedure depends on the person's **actual employment relationship**, contract, duties, and legal classification—not simply a job title or an HRMS label.

Specific provisions of the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, as amended through 2026**, apply to legally covered *workers*. Other employees may be governed by their enforceable contracts and other applicable law. No company policy can reduce a statutory entitlement.

A voluntary resignation is administered under the separate **Resignation & Notice Period Policy**. Completion of a genuine internship or independent-contractor engagement follows its lawful agreement and relevant Blinto SOPs; it must not be mislabeled to avoid employment rights.

## Who Makes the Decision

Blinto's management-approved separation approval chain is:

**Department Head recommendation → HR review → Chief of Staff final approval → HR communication.**

The Department Head identifies the concern or business reason and provides relevant evidence. HR checks the classification, correct legal route, fairness, notice, financial entitlements and documentation. **No Department Head may terminate an employee independently.** The **Chief of Staff** provides final internal authorization before an employer-initiated employment separation is executed, subject to any additional approval required by applicable law. HR communicates an approved decision privately and in writing.

A serious immediate safety, security, or client-risk situation may require temporary protective steps, such as removing dangerous access or changing work assignments. Such steps are **not a finding of misconduct or a completed termination**; lawful disciplinary or separation procedures and final approval still apply.

## Main Separation Categories

### 1. Employer-Initiated Termination — Section 26

For a worker covered by the Act, section 26 permits termination **other than through the specific dismissal, retrenchment, or discharge routes**, subject to the applicable legal safeguards and financial consequences. It does not always require an allegation of wrongdoing.

**Example:** After reviewing operations, Blinto decides it no longer wishes to continue an employment relationship. HR first checks whether section 26 is the appropriate route rather than using it to disguise a redundancy, disciplinary accusation, discrimination, or retaliation.

For covered workers, the statutory written notice periods under section 26 are generally:

| Worker classification under the Act | Employer notice |
| --- | ---: |
| Permanent, monthly-rated | **120 days** |
| Permanent, other than monthly-rated | **60 days** |
| Temporary, monthly-rated | **30 days** |
| Temporary, other than monthly-rated | **14 days**, subject to the statutory temporary-work exception |

**Blinto’s proposed 30-day working-notice arrangement:** For a permanent, monthly-rated worker, Blinto may ordinarily ask the employee to work a **30-day notice period** and pay **wages in lieu of the remaining statutory notice** (normally 90 days, if the full 120-day section 26 requirement applies), together with any separate compensation and other lawful dues. This does **not** reduce the statutory notice entitlement to 30 days. Any different lawful agreement or exemption must be verified by HR before implementation.

The employer may provide lawful wages in lieu of the required notice. For a permanent covered worker terminated under section 26, compensation is generally **30 days' wages per completed year of service, or gratuity if payable, whichever is higher**, in addition to other applicable dues. The statute's defined wage basis applies; it is not automatically identical to contractual gross salary.

The **30-day employee resignation notice** discussed in Blinto's resignation draft is **not** a substitute for these separate employer-notice requirements.

### 2. Disciplinary Dismissal — Sections 23 and 24

Disciplinary dismissal concerns **legally recognized misconduct**, not simply a disappointing performance review. Examples under section 23(4) include willful disobedience of a lawful and reasonable instruction, theft or fraud, bribery, habitual lateness, habitual negligence, disorderly conduct, repeated breaches of applicable rules, and falsifying or damaging official records.

**Example:** Blinto receives credible evidence that an employee falsified company records. HR must first establish the relevant facts and apply the required disciplinary procedure; an allegation alone does not establish guilt.

For a covered worker, before punishment under section 23, section 24 requires, as applicable:

- written allegations and delivery of a copy to the employee;
- **at least seven days to explain or answer formal statutory charges**; Blinto may request a response within **3 working days** only at a **preliminary clarification or non-statutory show-cause stage**;
- a meaningful opportunity to be heard;
- the legally required inquiry, including applicable committee-composition rules; and
- approval of the disciplinary order by the legally authorized employer or manager, together with Blinto's internal final approval.

**Blinto's 3-working-day response rule** may be used for an **initial HR clarification or preliminary show-cause inquiry** that is *not* the statutory formal charge response. It **must not shorten** a covered worker's formal response period under section 24. If an issue proceeds to formal statutory disciplinary charges, HR must issue the appropriate notice and allow **at least seven days**, even if the employee already answered an earlier three-working-day inquiry.

If serious misconduct is established through the prescribed process, dismissal without notice or notice pay may be lawful in applicable cases. However, whether dismissal, removal, or another punishment is appropriate—and whether compensation remains due—depends on the actual section 23 provision and the **2026 amendments**. HR must verify these consequences before issuing a final letter.

### 3. Retrenchment for Redundancy — Section 20

Retrenchment is used when a role or services become **surplus to the business's genuine needs**. It is **not discipline** and must not be presented as employee misconduct.

**Example:** Blinto restructures a function and needs two positions where it previously needed three. If one role genuinely becomes surplus, HR considers the statutory retrenchment route and applicable selection and re-employment protections.

For an eligible covered worker with at least one year of continuous service, section 20 generally requires **one month's written notice explaining the reason, or wages in lieu**, prescribed copies to the appropriate labour authority and any applicable collective bargaining agent, and compensation of **30 days' wages per completed year or gratuity if payable, whichever is higher**. Statutory selection rules and re-employment priority under section 21 may also apply. HR must verify any exceptions and requirements for the particular case.

### 4. Medical Discharge — Section 22

Medical discharge is distinct from misconduct and poor performance. For a covered worker, section 22 concerns **physical or mental incapacity or continued ill health certified by a registered medical practitioner**.

**Example:** An employee's medical condition materially affects their ability to continue the job. Blinto cannot infer incapacity solely from absences, a diagnosis, or managerial opinion. HR reviews legally required medical certification, applicable leave and other rights, and whether a lawful alternative is available before considering discharge.

Where a covered worker has completed at least one year of continuous service, section 22 generally provides **30 days' wages per completed year of service or gratuity if payable, whichever is higher**. Medical information must be handled under the Employee Data Privacy & Records Management Policy, with restricted disclosure.

### 5. Completion of Genuine Temporary Work or Engagement

Genuine temporary work may end when the specific work is completed, stopped, discontinued or otherwise ends in the manner permitted by law. Fixed-term engagements or internships may also reach an agreed endpoint, subject to actual legal classification and applicable rights.

**Example:** A person was legitimately hired for a clearly defined temporary project that is now complete. HR checks the actual arrangement and statutory rules before classifying the exit; an ordinary permanent employee should not be reclassified as temporary merely because a project ended.

### 6. Probation Outcome

An unsuccessful probation evaluation must follow the **Probation, Evaluation & Confirmation Policy**. The Department Head documents the role-related assessment; HR verifies the **actual legal employment status**, permitted probation or extension terms, and applicable notice and dues. Chief of Staff final internal approval applies to employer-initiated separation.

A late confirmation letter or incorrect HRMS status does not cancel a legal status already acquired under applicable law. Blinto will not assume that every probationary employee can be separated without notice or due process.

### 7. Mutual Written Separation

Blinto and an employee may, where legally permissible, agree in writing to end the employment relationship. An agreement must be voluntary, clear about the effective date and financial treatment, and must not be used to force the waiver of non-waivable statutory rights.

## Poor Performance Is Not Automatically Misconduct

Underperformance—such as missed deadlines, poor-quality work, or unmet expectations—should normally be addressed through the **Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy**: clear feedback, evidence-based coaching, and an appropriate opportunity to improve, including a PIP when suitable.

**Example:** An employee repeatedly misses project targets despite coaching. HR reviews the documented performance history and possible next steps; it must not automatically call the situation theft, insubordination or statutory misconduct. If the employment relationship must end, HR evaluates a lawful route such as section 26 where applicable.

If separate evidence supports *habitual negligence* or another legally defined form of misconduct, HR may assess sections 23–24, but only through the required process. A performance-management label cannot be used to avoid disciplinary safeguards or statutory payments.

## Notice, Compensation, and Final Dues

Before issuing the final separation letter, HR and Finance determine the relevant legal route, correct notice or payment in lieu, any route-specific compensation or gratuity, unpaid wages, eligible accrued leave, outstanding approved payments, and legally permitted deductions.

**The amount due is not identical for every type of exit.** Section 26, retrenchment, medical discharge, misconduct-related punishment and other separations may have different rules. HR and Finance must check the law in force at the time and the employee's stronger enforceable contractual rights, if any.

Final dues follow the separate **Final Settlement & Employee Dues Policy** and statutory deadlines. Pay may **not be arbitrarily withheld because of incomplete clearance, asset disputes, or pending handover**. Any lawful claim relating to property or notice must be handled according to the applicable rules.

## Communication and Employee Rights

HR communicates the decision privately, respectfully and in writing. The letter must correctly identify the type of separation and the effective employment date and state reasons where the applicable route requires them. Employees will be told how to seek clarification about notice, dues and next steps.

Internal communication should identify only what colleagues need for continuity and security; it should not publicize sensitive allegations, medical details, or unnecessary personal information.

Employees may provide relevant information to HR, challenge errors, and request review by uninvolved authorized senior leadership where appropriate. This internal process does **not** replace statutory grievance or labour-court rights, including applicable time limits under section 33.

Blinto must not use separation as a disguise for unlawful discrimination, retaliation, or punishment for raising a protected complaint.

## Offboarding and Records

Once lawful separation is approved and communicated, employees and responsible teams follow **Employee Offboarding** for work handover, property return, and timely removal of access. HR and Finance complete final payments under the relevant policy. Service certificates or other employment documents are provided where legally required.

HR records the **actual legal separation reason**, rather than treating all exits as simply “terminated.” Employee records remain confidential under the Employee Data Privacy & Records Management Policy.

### Recommended HRMS Exit Reasons (Proposed)

Until these categories are implemented and validated in HRMS, HR should maintain the correct separation classification in the approved controlled employment record:

| Proposed HRMS exit reason | Employment meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Voluntary Resignation | Employee-initiated departure; separate Resignation Policy |
| Employer-Initiated Termination — Section 26 | Applicable ordinary statutory employer termination |
| Disciplinary Dismissal — Sections 23–24 | Misconduct or conviction, as applicable, with prescribed procedure |
| Retrenchment — Section 20 | Genuine role or workforce redundancy |
| Medical Discharge — Section 22 | Certified incapacity or continued ill health |
| Genuine Temporary Work / Contract Completion | Actual lawful end of temporary or applicable fixed-duration work |
| Probation Outcome | Separate recording of legally valid probation-related separation |
| Mutual Written Separation | Voluntary lawful separation agreement |

These are **proposed HRMS options**, not claims that the HRMS already provides them. An exit category does not itself establish that a statutory section applies to a particular employee.

## Responsibilities

**Department Head:** Identify and document the operational concern; make a recommendation, but do not independently terminate employment.

**People Operations / HR:** Verify legal classification and separation route; ensure correct procedure, fair communication and accurate records; coordinate review and documentation.

**CEO:** Provide final internal approval for employer-initiated separation, consistent with the existing Offboarding SOP.

**Finance:** Verify and process lawful notice amounts, compensation and final dues with HR, including statutory deadlines.

**Employee:** May respond to allegations, request clarification, present relevant information, and exercise applicable legal and contractual rights.

## Legal and Management Review Note

This is a **draft for management and Bangladesh legal review**, not a substitute for individual case advice. The cited provisions are the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, as amended through **April 2026**, particularly sections 20–24, 26, 31 and 33. Rules for statutory *workers* must not be mechanically applied to legally excluded roles; equally, a managerial-sounding title alone does not decide coverage.

**Required distinction:** Blinto's **3-working-day initial HR response target** is preserved, but the legally required **minimum seven-day reply period** applies to formal section 24 disciplinary charges against a covered worker.

## Approval Alignment Note

**Internal approval coordination:** This policy assigns Chief of Staff approval, whereas the separately approved Employee Offboarding SOP specifies CEO final approval for involuntary termination. These documents require an aligned written delegation before executing an involuntary separation. People Operations must escalate any such conflict and obtain the required authorized decision.
