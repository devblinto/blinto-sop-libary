# Attendance Correction & Exception Handling SOP

## Purpose

Explain how Blinto employees can correct attendance records or request an attendance exception, and how HR reviews, approves, documents and communicates those decisions fairly.

This SOP **does not change** the approved [Attendance Policy](./attendance-policy.md): that policy determines punctuality, the monthly late allowance and HRMS warning stages. This document governs **requests, verification, corrections, exceptions and disputes**.

## Scope

This SOP applies to employees whose attendance is administered through Blinto's HRMS and related approved records. It covers:

- missing check-ins or check-outs;
- inaccurate arrival, departure or attendance times;
- HRMS faults, access failures and connectivity problems;
- approved leave recorded incorrectly as absence;
- approved work from home, off-site work or business travel recorded incorrectly;
- approved schedule changes and accommodations;
- emergencies affecting arrival, departure or attendance; and
- disputed late counts, reminders or automatically generated warnings.

This is not an alternative way to request new leave, WFH approval or overtime after the fact. Those matters remain governed by their own policies.

# Part A — Employee Guidance

## If Your Attendance Record Is Wrong

Tell **HR** and inform your **Department Head or Team Lead** when you discover an incorrect record.

Where the current HRMS supports attendance correction requests, use that feature. **If the feature does not exist, is unavailable or cannot handle the situation, contact HR directly using an approved work channel.** Employees must not edit their own official attendance records informally or ask another employee to do so.

Explain the affected date, the check-in or check-out time believed to be correct, and what happened. Share relevant supporting context when HR reasonably requests it. You do not need to disclose unnecessary medical or private personal information.

**Timing:** Report the issue **on the same working day where practical**, and normally **within three working days**. A genuine emergency, technical problem or other justified circumstance may support a later request. A company request window never overrides a legal wage, leave or record-correction entitlement.

Examples:

- You worked as usual but forgot to press **check-out**: notify HR, identify the actual end time and provide available work context.
- HRMS shows **absence** despite approved leave: refer HR to the approved leave record.
- Your approved **emergency WFH** day appears as an office absence: ask HR to reconcile it with the WFH authorization and applicable attendance process.
- An HRMS error creates a **late warning**: report it promptly; a warning based on a wrong record can be reviewed and withdrawn.

A missing check-in **does not automatically prove** that the employee failed to work.

## What Happens After You Report It

1. **HR reviews the record** and asks for clarification or proportionate supporting information if needed.
2. **The Department Head confirms** actual work, approved arrangements or operational context where relevant.
3. **HR decides routine corrections** and arranges authorized record updates. Exceptional or unresolved disputed cases go to the **Chief of Staff**.
4. **HR communicates the outcome** and, where appropriate, explains a refusal or requested revision.
5. **HR coordinates any consequences** for late counts, leave balances or payroll records with the responsible owner.

Employees may explain a disagreement, correct factual misunderstandings and ask for a Chief of Staff review when a material dispute remains unresolved. A good-faith request for correction is **not misconduct**.

## When an Exception May Be Approved

An exception may be appropriate for verified HRMS outages or device failures, approved leave or WFH, authorized off-site work or business travel, a medical or family emergency, or another unavoidable event reasonably assessed on its facts.

An everyday personal delay does **not** automatically qualify as an exception. HR evaluates requests consistently and considers documented authorization, relevant facts and any legal or contractual protections.

## What Stays the Same Under the Attendance Policy

Blinto's approved normal arrival window remains **8:30–8:45 AM**. Arrival after **8:45 AM** is treated as late unless an approved exception applies.

The approved automatic late allowance remains **two days per calendar month**, with each late arrival limited to **up to one hour**. The existing monthly HRMS sequence is unchanged:

| Late event in the month | Approved attendance treatment |
| --- | --- |
| #1 | Automatic late allowance |
| #2 | Automatic late allowance |
| #3 | Automatic reminder to employee |
| #4 | Coaching/escalation notification to employee, Team/Department Lead and HR |
| #5 | Formal attendance warning recorded in HRMS |
| #6 onward | HR and manager review under the Corrective Action Policy |

The count resets each calendar month; historical records remain available for legitimate attendance review. **Approved exceptions and corrected false late events are excluded from the relevant count.** This SOP introduces **no new financial penalty or extra late allowance**.

## Pay, Leave and Privacy

A disputed or missing HRMS record is not, by itself, permission to withhold earned wages, impose a deduction or reduce statutory leave. HR must establish the actual circumstances and follow the law and the applicable payroll or leave policy.

Information provided for a correction is used for legitimate attendance administration and related payroll or leave reconciliation. HR should restrict access to authorized people and avoid collecting excessive personal data.

# Part B — Internal HR & Management Procedure

## 1. Receive and Identify the Request

HR receives correction requests through the supported HRMS workflow **where available**, or directly through an approved work channel if necessary.

HR identifies the employee, date, attendance entry in dispute, proposed correct status or time, stated reason and any related approved leave/WFH/schedule arrangement.

If the employee reports an ongoing HRMS fault, HR should ensure the concern reaches the authorized system administrator or system owner. Do not describe unconfirmed HRMS functionality as implemented.

## 2. Triage Urgent and Payroll-Sensitive Cases

HR gives appropriate priority to cases involving an imminent payroll close, wrongful leave deduction, an inaccurate formal warning, multiple affected employees or a suspected technical outage.

Where payroll processing has already closed, HR records and coordinates a lawful adjustment rather than silently changing a final payroll figure or postponing legally owed payments.

A request made after three working days may still be considered where justified; statutory rights are not extinguished by this administrative target.

## 3. Verify the Relevant Facts

HR checks the available evidence appropriate to the case, which may include:

- HRMS timestamps, audit details or error information **if available**;
- existing approved leave, WFH, off-site work or schedule records;
- Department Head or Team Lead confirmation;
- relevant ClickUp work activity, assignment records or authorized meeting information; and
- the employee's explanation and other relevant material.

A digital log may support verification but should not be treated as conclusive where reasonable contrary evidence exists. Do not demand unrelated private messages, excessive health details or irrelevant personal information.

**Department Heads verify** actual work and previously approved arrangements; they do not grant themselves authority to rewrite official HR attendance or payroll records.

## 4. Determine the Correct Status

HR distinguishes among:

- a genuine record error where work occurred;
- approved leave, WFH, off-site work or schedule exception;
- qualifying emergency or technical failure;
- actual lateness, early departure or absence; and
- an incomplete or disputed case that requires further review.

HR must not treat a retrospective record correction as automatic approval of overtime, previously unapproved leave or other separate entitlements. Those decisions are handled under the appropriate governing policy.

If there is evidence of deliberate falsification, HR should handle the concern separately through the established fair corrective-action or investigation procedure rather than automatically rejecting every related factual correction.

## 5. Approve and Apply an Authorized Correction

**Routine attendance corrections:** HR provides final approval after review, with Department Head verification where relevant.

**Exceptional or unresolved disputed cases:** Chief of Staff reviews and provides final internal determination, subject to applicable legal rights and any required independent process.

**Technical changes:** An authorized HRMS administrator may implement an approved correction, but technical access alone is **not approval authority**. No employee may authorize their own attendance change.

HR should preserve a traceable record of the original issue, information considered, decision, approving person, adjustment, date and any employee communication. This may be held in the current authorized HRMS **if supported**, otherwise in a suitably restricted official HR record. Do not overwrite or delete required evidence without an appropriate audit trail.

## 6. Correct Late Counts, Warnings and Leave Balances

Where a correction changes an attendance classification:

- update the underlying record through an authorized method;
- exclude approved exceptions and incorrect late events from the monthly late count;
- recalculate the relevant count and warning status when the system permits, or document and arrange an approved manual correction where it does not;
- review any reminder, coaching notification or formal warning triggered by the incorrect record;
- withdraw or amend an unjustified warning and communicate the result to the employee and relevant authorized recipients; and
- reconcile affected leave balances under the [Leave & Time-Off Policy](./leave-and-time-off-policy.md) where appropriate.

The approved [Attendance Policy](./attendance-policy.md) governs the monthly escalation sequence; this SOP only corrects its factual inputs and resulting errors.

## 7. Coordinate Payroll Without Creating Unauthorized Deductions

HR informs **Finance only where a correction affects pay or payroll records**. Finance verifies and implements adjustments under the approved compensation, payroll and final-settlement processes.

Bangladesh Labour Act **sections 125–126** restrict wage deductions for absence. For covered workers, a deduction for unauthorized absence may be made only on the legally permitted basis and cannot exceed the wages payable for the relevant period of absence, subject to applicable statutory provisions. **A missing check-in or unresolved HRMS alert alone is not proof of unauthorized absence.**

Where a correction demonstrates that work was performed or leave was properly approved, HR and Finance must correct any improper payroll treatment. Earned wages must be paid within applicable legal and contractual time limits; records disputes cannot be used as a blanket excuse to delay payment.

The employee's legal worker classification, terms and any more favorable statutory or contractual treatment must be assessed as applicable. Changes to overtime must be verified separately under the [Overtime Policy](./overtime-policy.md).

## 8. Explain the Decision and Handle a Dispute

HR informs the employee of the decision, relevant record adjustment or reasonable explanation for nonapproval. If a material concern remains unresolved, the employee may seek review by the **Chief of Staff**.

The Chief of Staff should consider the employee's explanation, the Department Head's verification, available records and any conflict of interest. Applicable legal grievance or wage-claim rights remain available and are not replaced by this internal review.

Good-faith questions and correction requests must not invite retaliation. Intentional misrepresentation, when supported by evidence, is addressed through a fair procedure under the [Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy](./corrective-action-and-performance-improvement-policy.md).

## 9. Maintain Confidential Records and Review Recurring Problems

HR retains correction evidence and approvals in an appropriately restricted location consistent with applicable retention duties. Only HR, authorized management, system administrators and Finance to the extent necessary should access the relevant information.

HR and the system owner should examine repeated forgotten check-ins, HRMS faults, unclear employee instructions, inconsistent Department Head verification and manual warning corrections to identify improvements.

This is an improvement expectation, **not a claim of an existing automated correction dashboard, payroll synchronization or audit workflow**.

## Responsibilities

**Employees:** Report mistakes promptly, provide accurate context, inform the Department Head and avoid unauthorized changes.

**Department Heads / Team Leads:** Verify actual work and approvals, address operational effects and coordinate with HR. They cannot approve their own correction.

**People Operations / HR:** Owns case review, routine approvals, confidential records, warning corrections, communication and payroll/leave coordination.

**Chief of Staff:** Resolves exceptional or materially disputed cases internally, without overriding mandatory legal protections.

**Finance:** Verifies and processes lawful payroll adjustments only when affected; does not infer an absence deduction from an unverified HRMS alert.

**Authorized system administrator:** Executes approved technical changes within granted permissions and preserves required record integrity.

## Current Practice and Proposed Improvements

**Existing approved rules:** HRMS is used for attendance administration and the approved Attendance Policy defines the monthly automatic late allowance and escalation. HR has authority under that policy to correct or withdraw unjustified automated warnings.

**Reported current problems:** Forgotten check-ins/check-outs; technical errors; uncertainty about the correction route and approvals; automatic late warnings requiring adjustment; inconsistent Department Head verification; imperfect payroll/HRMS reconciliation; and no standalone correction SOP.

**Proposed improvements in this draft:** An employee-friendly correction route, a three-working-day normal reporting expectation, clearly allocated HR and Chief of Staff approvals, proportionate evidence review, traceable authorized changes, and more reliable payroll/leave reconciliation.

No statement here assumes that a specific HRMS correction form, self-service request function, automated reprocessing feature or integration already exists.

## Legal Considerations

The SOP should be applied with the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, as amended through 2026**, and applicable **Bangladesh Labour Rules, 2015, as amended**, including:

- **Section 9:** relevant statutory worker-register duties;
- **Sections 121–123:** responsibility for and timing of payment of wages;
- **Sections 125–126:** permitted wage deductions and limits on absence deductions; and
- applicable attendance, overtime and leave record requirements prescribed by the Labour Rules, including relevant daily-attendance and overtime records.

HR and Finance should verify current applicability and required record formats based on legal worker classification and the establishment's circumstances. This SOP does not authorize changing a statutory record without a proper audit trail, withholding earned wages, or substituting an HRMS status for legal proof.
