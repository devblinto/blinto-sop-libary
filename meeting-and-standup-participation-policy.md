# Meeting & Standup Participation Policy

## Purpose

Help Blinto employees participate in meetings and daily stand-ups effectively, respectfully, and on time. Meetings should support clear priorities, useful decisions, and coordinated work without taking more time than necessary.

This policy explains **how to participate**. The approved [Office Hours Policy](./office-hours-policy.md) establishes the daily stand-up schedule and normal working hours. The approved [Attendance Policy](./attendance-policy.md) governs attendance records and lateness; this policy does not create a separate attendance penalty.

## Who This Policy Applies To

This policy applies to confirmed and probationary employees, paid interns, Department Heads and team leaders, and employees attending Blinto or client meetings. Other invited participants should follow the applicable meeting expectations when working with Blinto, subject to their engagement arrangements and client rules.

## Daily Stand-Up: 9:00–9:15 AM

Blinto's daily stand-up **starts at 9:00 AM and normally ends by 9:15 AM**, as established by the approved Office Hours Policy. Relevant employees are expected to participate unless excused or covered by an approved absence or other arrangement.

Employees should prepare **before 9:00 AM** by reviewing their current work in ClickUp and identifying key progress, priorities, and blockers. When called on, each participant should briefly explain:

- **Previous progress:** What was completed or materially advanced since the prior update.
- **Today's plan:** The main assignments or priorities for the day.
- **Blockers:** Anything that needs another person's input, approval, or attention.

Updates should be concise and relevant. The stand-up is for team coordination, **not an extended troubleshooting or problem-solving session**. Detailed discussions should move to a follow-up conversation with the people directly involved.

The **Department Head or assigned lead** facilitates the stand-up and helps identify issues requiring follow-up. Employees should not wait for the next stand-up to report an urgent blocker or risk.

### Breakfast and Stand-Up Priority

Blinto's scheduled team breakfast ends at **8:45 AM**, while the daily stand-up begins promptly at **9:00 AM**. Employees should normally finish breakfast during the scheduled breakfast period and use the time before 9:00 AM to prepare for stand-up.

**Missing the scheduled breakfast does not excuse a participant from the 9:00 AM stand-up.** If a required participant misses breakfast or arrives too late to finish eating before stand-up, they must **attend stand-up at 9:00 AM** instead of going to eat during the meeting. **After stand-up ends at approximately 9:15 AM, they may eat the missed breakfast**, coordinating briefly with their lead so that the delayed meal does not create another uncommunicated work absence.

**Shared accountability applies in this situation:**

- **The participant** is responsible for prioritizing stand-up attendance, preparing their update, and communicating any genuine obstacle or emergency.
- **The Team Lead / Department Head** is responsible for reinforcing the stand-up schedule, directing the participant to join on time, and addressing repeated avoidable conflicts.
- **HR / People Operations** is responsible for communicating this expectation, supporting consistent application, and reviewing any repeated noncompliance fairly under the existing attendance or conduct process.

Accountability here means responsibility for following and administering the rule; it **does not create an automatic disciplinary finding, a new financial penalty, or shared personal liability** for the Team Lead or HR. Approved leave, emergencies, medical needs, or other reasonable accommodations must still be considered under the applicable policies.

## Attendance and Absences

Attendance at a required meeting or stand-up is expected unless the participant has been excused or has a valid reason for absence.

Employees should:

- join at the scheduled time and remain for the relevant discussion;
- notify the organizer or responsible lead as soon as reasonably possible if they cannot attend or expect to be late;
- treat approved leave, emergencies, unavoidable client obligations, and genuine technical difficulties as circumstances requiring reasonable consideration; and
- inform the organizer before leaving a required meeting early, unless an emergency makes advance notice impractical.

**Meeting participation is not a replacement for HRMS attendance or leave records.** Attendance-related matters are governed by the approved Attendance Policy.

Repeated unexplained absence from required meetings may be referred to HR for fair review. An absence should not be presumed to be misconduct before the circumstances are considered.

## Punctuality and Late Arrival

Participants and organizers should be ready at the scheduled start and respect the planned end time.

If an employee arrives after a meeting begins, they should join without interrupting and obtain missed information afterward when needed. Repeated lateness should be discussed with the responsible lead, taking account of unavoidable client, technical, or personal emergencies and any approved exceptions.

**Stand-up lateness follows Blinto's approved Attendance and Office Hours policies.** No separate meeting-specific fine, automatic payroll deduction, or new financial penalty is created by this policy.

## Preparing for Meetings

Before attending a stand-up or other work meeting, employees should understand the purpose of the discussion and come ready to contribute.

Preparation may include reviewing relevant **ClickUp tasks**, current progress, project documents, known blockers, upcoming commitments, and any agenda shared by the organizer. Team members should raise foreseeable schedule conflicts early instead of missing a meeting without notice.

The organizer should make the meeting's purpose clear, invite only participants whose involvement is useful, and provide enough context for meaningful preparation when practical.

## Respectful Meeting Conduct

Every participant is expected to:

- speak professionally and respectfully, including when disagreeing;
- listen without unnecessary interruptions and allow others to contribute;
- keep contributions relevant to the meeting objective;
- avoid unrelated conversations, phone use, and distracting notifications;
- protect sensitive company, client, and employee information; and
- give constructive feedback without publicly embarrassing or attacking colleagues.

Sensitive concerns about conduct, harassment, discrimination, or individual employment matters should be handled through the relevant confidential HR or grievance process, not discussed in an inappropriate group meeting.

## Scheduling and Running Other Meetings

Meetings should have a clear business purpose, reasonable duration, and the necessary participants. Organizers should consider employees' working schedules, project deadlines, and client commitments, and use **Google Meet or another approved meeting tool** for online meetings.

Organizers are responsible for clarifying significant outcomes, next steps, and assigned owners. Participants should not assume a discussion has changed a project deadline or responsibility until the relevant owner has communicated or documented the decision.

Employees and organizers should aim to start and finish on time. A meeting may run longer when genuinely necessary, but avoid allowing routine discussions to expand without a clear purpose.

## Recording Decisions and Action Items

**Important decisions, task changes, and action items must be recorded in ClickUp or another approved company system.** A verbal instruction or conversation alone should not be the only record of a material change to project scope, delivery timing, or task ownership.

Where relevant, the record should make clear:

- the decision or action required;
- the responsible person or team; and
- any agreed deadline, priority, or change to scope.

The organizer should ensure someone is responsible for capturing material outcomes; this does **not** require formal minutes for every short stand-up.

Sensitive HR, performance, disciplinary, or confidential client details must not be posted in broadly accessible ClickUp tasks or group spaces. Use an appropriately restricted and authorized location.

The drafted [Workplace Communication & Collaboration Policy](./workplace-communication-and-collaboration-policy.md) governs how Telegram, ClickUp, Gmail, and other communication channels are used together. **Telegram is for regular communication; ClickUp remains the source of truth for assigned tasks and material project updates.**

## Virtual and Client Meetings

For online or client meetings, employees should:

- use the approved meeting platform or the client's authorized platform;
- check audio, connectivity, and access ahead of time where practical;
- join from an environment suitable for professional discussion and confidentiality;
- check screen-sharing content to avoid accidentally exposing unrelated client files, credentials, or private information;
- avoid recording the meeting without the required authorization and any applicable notice or consent; and
- follow client-specific meeting and participation requirements that have been appropriately communicated.

Unavoidable technical problems should be reported promptly to the organizer or project owner. This policy does not impose a blanket camera-on requirement or presume access to special meeting software.

### Meetings Outside Normal Working Hours

International client meetings may occasionally need to occur outside Blinto's standard schedule, but such arrangements require the appropriate approval and coordination.

Any additional work outside normal working hours is subject to the applicable [Overtime Policy](./overtime-policy.md), employment terms, and legal requirements. Scheduling a meeting does not, by itself, waive statutory or contractual compensation rights.

## Responsibilities

**Employees and interns:** Prepare for required meetings, join on time—including stand-up when breakfast was missed—share concise and accurate updates, communicate unavoidable absence or lateness, and complete assigned follow-up actions.

**Department Heads and assigned leads:** Facilitate effective stand-ups, reinforce the rule that delayed breakfast must not displace the 9:00 AM stand-up, clarify priorities and blockers, consider legitimate scheduling conflicts, and support fair resolution of repeated attendance or participation concerns.

**Meeting organizers and project owners:** Schedule purposeful meetings, communicate outcomes and owners, and ensure material project decisions are recorded in ClickUp or the approved project system.

**People Operations / HR:** Explain the policy and breakfast/stand-up priority during relevant onboarding, support consistent application, and review repeated unexplained absences or conduct concerns under the applicable policies.

## Current Practice and Proposed Improvements

**Confirmed existing practices:** Blinto's approved Office Hours Policy already establishes a **9:00–9:15 AM daily stand-up**, including updates on previous work, today's priorities, and blockers. The approved Attendance Policy governs lateness and attendance administration. ClickUp is used for task management.

**Problems identified:** Employees sometimes arrive late to stand-up, miss meetings without informing the organizer, and lack a dedicated employee-facing meeting policy.

**Approved standards in this policy:** Clearer expectations for notice of absence, preparation, punctuality, respectful participation, recording material meeting decisions, and fair handling of repeated unexplained absences. These approved standards do not claim that new attendance or monitoring software has already been deployed.

## Questions and Concerns

Employees should ask the Department Head or relevant meeting organizer if they are unsure whether a meeting is required, who should attend, or how to record the outcome. They may raise concerns about unfair attendance treatment or inappropriate meeting conduct with HR through the relevant confidential process.
