# Exit Interview & Employee Turnover Analysis SOP

## Purpose

Help Blinto learn from employee departures, understand recurring reasons for turnover, and improve employee experience, management, retention and workplace practices. Exit interviews are opportunities to share feedback, not tests an employee must pass before leaving.

This SOP covers voluntary exit feedback and internal analysis. Employment separation, handover, employment documents and final payments remain governed by the applicable offboarding, resignation, termination and final-settlement policies.

## Who This Applies To

People Operations may invite the following departing team members to participate, as appropriate:

- Employees resigning voluntarily or completing fixed-term employment;
- Employees leaving after probation, including Department Heads and management;
- Employees in company-initiated separations, where an interview is appropriate and safe;
- Paid interns completing their internship.

**Participation is voluntary.** HR may omit an interview when a sensitive situation makes it inappropriate. A departing employee may decline, skip a subject, or request another mutually convenient time. No negative inference or employment consequence follows simply from declining.

## Employee Guidance

### 1. Invitation and timing

HR will normally offer an interview during the notice period, ideally on or near the last working day at a mutually convenient time. An alternative time can be arranged when practical. The interview is separate from mandatory handover or exit administration.

You do not need to attend an exit interview to complete offboarding, receive required documents, or receive salary, leave payments or other lawful final dues. HR and Finance must follow the separate final-settlement process and applicable legal deadlines regardless of interview participation.

### 2. What you may discuss

You may share your experience of:

- Your main reasons for leaving and whether you would consider returning;
- Job satisfaction, role clarity, onboarding and performance feedback;
- Management support, leadership, teamwork and communication;
- Workload, office hours and work-life balance;
- Compensation, benefits, learning and career progression;
- Workplace respect, safety and culture;
- Practical suggestions for improving Blinto.

You may choose not to answer a particular question. Provide feedback candidly and respectfully; factual examples can help HR understand concerns, but supporting documents are not required for ordinary opinions or suggestions.

### 3. How your feedback is handled

HR will explain the purpose of collecting feedback, who may need to review it, and how it may be used. Individual comments will not be circulated widely. Identifiable information will be shared only where there is a legitimate business, safety, complaint-resolution or legal reason.

**Absolute anonymity cannot be guaranteed.** A concern involving alleged harassment, discrimination, fraud, immediate safety risks or another serious matter may need a separate fair review. HR will handle disclosures with appropriate discretion and will not assume an allegation is proven simply because it was reported.

Sensitive exit comments must not be placed in open ClickUp tasks, general Telegram groups or other unrestricted channels. You may contact HR separately if you prefer to discuss a sensitive matter outside the ordinary interview.

### 4. Complaints and unresolved employment matters

You may report a grievance or request a separate formal complaint review, including after choosing not to attend the exit interview. HR will route harassment, discrimination and workplace-respect concerns through the applicable grievance policy; safety or information-security concerns will be escalated promptly; payroll or final-dues concerns will be referred to the responsible HR/Finance process.

The Chief of Staff reviews unresolved or significant concerns under the applicable escalation arrangements. Any employment or disciplinary decision follows its own fair process and authorization requirements. Good-faith reporting must not trigger retaliation.

## Internal HR Procedure

### 1. Identify a suitable exit interview

After HR receives an approved separation or internship-completion notice, determine whether an interview is appropriate. Consider the departure type, relevant safety or employee-relations risks, confidentiality and the departing person's preferences. Do not require participation, and do not use an interview to obtain a waiver of statutory rights.

### 2. Select the interviewer and arrange a time

People Operations normally conducts the interview. Where practicable, use an HR representative who is not personally involved in the concern. The direct manager does not automatically conduct the interview and may provide operational context separately.

If HR is implicated in a complaint or cannot provide a neutral process, arrange an authorized independent reviewer. Explain the interview's purpose and the voluntary nature of participation before starting.

### 3. Conduct a respectful, balanced discussion

Invite feedback about the employee's experience, why they are leaving and what Blinto could improve. Use open-ended questions and let the employee control how much detail to provide. Avoid pressure, argument, promises of an outcome, or requests for unnecessary private information.

Separate reported facts, employee perceptions and HR observations in the record. Do not characterize unverified allegations as findings. Correct obvious recording inaccuracies when identified.

### 4. Securely record and classify feedback

HR maintains a restricted record of whether the interview was offered, held or declined; the broad departure reason; relevant themes; and any matters requiring follow-up. Record identifiable details only where needed. Use existing authorized HR-controlled storage, not a presumed new exit-interview platform or centralized analytics tool.

Give access only to authorized personnel with a legitimate need. Retain and dispose of records according to the Employee Data Privacy & Records Management Policy and applicable legal, contractual and preservation requirements; this SOP does not establish a new fixed retention period.

Where small team sizes or unusual circumstances could identify a former employee, suppress or generalize details in broader reporting.

### 5. Route material concerns appropriately

Handle the content according to its nature:

- **Workplace harassment, discrimination or grievance:** apply the relevant workplace grievance and fair-review process.
- **Immediate safety or information-security risk:** notify the responsible operational function promptly and preserve relevant information.
- **Salary, leave balance or final dues:** coordinate with HR and Finance under Payroll Processing and Final Settlement policies.
- **Serious or unresolved organizational concern:** escalate to the Chief of Staff; inform the CEO where the nature and authorization of the matter require it.

Keep any investigation or formal complaint separate from ordinary turnover statistics. A departure interview does not replace statutory complaint procedures or an employee's right to respond in a formal case.

### 6. Update the employee departure record

For each exit, HR verifies the departure date and relevant status from the governing employment record, distinguishing voluntary resignation, completion of term/internship and company-initiated separation. Record broad departure reasons as available and indicate when the reason is unknown or the interview was declined. Do not invent a reason to fill a gap.

The official separation and offboarding records remain the source for factual dates and employment status; an interview comment does not itself amend those records.

### 7. Analyze turnover trends

After each departure, HR updates the existing restricted departure record. Prepare **quarterly and annual turnover summaries** with a proportionate level of detail. Where data supports meaningful analysis, consider:

- Voluntary versus company-initiated departures;
- Departure reasons and recurring feedback themes;
- Department and role patterns, without exposing individuals;
- Length of service, including probation-related departures;
- Relevant existing onboarding, performance and workplace information.

When calculating turnover rates, use a clearly stated period, denominator and headcount method consistently. Small or incomplete datasets should be labelled as limited rather than treated as conclusive patterns. No fixed turnover target, analytics software or automated dashboard is introduced by this SOP.

### 8. Share findings and assign improvements

Provide quarterly and annual summaries to the Chief of Staff, with significant organizational findings escalated to the CEO. Department Heads receive only relevant findings in an anonymized or otherwise appropriately restricted form.

HR identifies recurring themes and recommends practical changes. The Chief of Staff decides significant cross-company initiatives; Department Heads contribute team-specific actions; and an appropriate owner is assigned to each approved improvement. HR periodically reviews whether earlier changes addressed the concern.

Do not promise the departing employee that a suggested change will be adopted before management approval. Exit feedback does not automatically initiate corrective action against any person.

### 9. Close the interview activity without holding up offboarding

HR confirms any follow-up owner, limits access to interview records, and coordinates with the approved Employee Offboarding procedure. Offboarding, service documentation and final settlement continue independently even if feedback was declined, rescheduled, or requires further review.

## Responsibilities

| Role | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| Departing employee or intern | May participate voluntarily, share feedback or raise a separate concern |
| People Operations / HR | Offers interviews, conducts neutral discussions, protects records, tracks departure reasons and prepares turnover analysis |
| Department Head | Provides work-related context separately and acts on approved team improvements |
| Neutral authorized reviewer | Handles an interview or complaint where HR involvement creates a conflict |
| Finance | Addresses referred pay or final-dues matters under the governing policy |
| Chief of Staff | Reviews significant or unresolved issues and oversees material company-wide actions |
| CEO | Receives significant organizational findings and participates only where existing decision authority requires it |

## Fairness, Privacy and Legal Safeguards

- Exit interviews are voluntary and must not condition an employee's separation, lawful payments, certificates or other employment rights.
- Personal information and identifiable feedback are collected and shared only for legitimate purposes, protected using appropriate access controls, and retained only as required under the governing privacy and recordkeeping rules.
- When a concern may require formal investigation, follow the relevant grievance, corrective-action and lawful employment procedures; preserve fairness, a meaningful opportunity to respond where applicable, and protection against retaliation.
- Statutory employment-record and final-dues obligations remain separate from this interview process. HR and Finance must apply the law and the applicable employee classification rather than treating this SOP as authority to delay or reduce any entitlement.
