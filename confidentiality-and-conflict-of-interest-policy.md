# Confidentiality & Conflict of Interest Policy

## Purpose

Explain how Blinto team members protect confidential information, handle outside employment and personal interests fairly, and disclose possible conflicts before they affect a business decision. The goal is to protect company and client trust without unnecessarily restricting lawful personal activities.

## Scope

This policy applies to confirmed and probationary employees, paid interns, freelancers, consultants, and other authorized people who handle Blinto or client confidential information. Applicable NDAs, client agreements, employment or engagement terms, and laws must also be followed.

## What Is Confidential?

Confidential information includes nonpublic company or client information, including:

- client files, project details, unpublished source code, designs, deliverables and business information;
- client credentials, account access and protected technical details;
- employee personnel, salary, medical, identity and contact records;
- Blinto financial records, budgets, pricing, proposals, business strategies and trade secrets; and
- nonpublic management decisions, internal meetings and operational information.

A file is not public merely because a team member can access it. Publicly available material and disclosures permitted or required by law are not prohibited simply by this policy.

## Everyday Confidentiality Responsibilities

Access information only when necessary for authorized work. Keep confidential records in approved company-controlled locations and share them only with people who have appropriate access and a legitimate work need.

Do not forward company or client information to personal email or unauthorized storage, discuss confidential matters where outsiders can overhear, disclose company or client secrets, or upload protected information to unapproved AI tools. Avoid unauthorized copies and report accidental disclosure or suspected misuse promptly to HR or the responsible system owner.

Detailed password, authentication and account-sharing rules belong to [Credential & Password Management](./credential-and-password-management-policy.md). Permission grants and their recording belong to [Access Provisioning & Deprovisioning](./access-provisioning-and-deprovisioning.md).

## Confidentiality Agreements and Duties After Leaving

Employees sign the applicable NDA or confidentiality agreement during onboarding. Interns, freelancers and consultants follow applicable engagement and confidentiality terms.

Confidentiality duties continue after employment or engagement **to the extent required by enforceable agreements and law**. On departure, company and client work information must be properly returned or transferred through the [Employee Offboarding](./employee-offboarding.md) process. Departing people must not retain unauthorized copies, credentials, or restricted client materials.

Nothing in this policy prohibits lawful reporting to authorities, protected disclosures, participation in legal proceedings, or use of public information and general professional skills. It does not establish an automatic industry-wide or post-employment non-compete prohibition.

## Outside Employment, Freelance Work, and Personal Activities

Lawful personal projects and outside work are not automatically prohibited. However, they must not interfere with Blinto work, misuse Blinto resources, expose confidential information, or create an unmanaged conflict.

**Prior written approval** is required before an employee works for a **direct competitor** or privately serves an existing **Blinto client** where the work overlaps or conflicts with Blinto's relationship or business interests. A proposed outside engagement creating an actual or reasonably foreseeable conflict must be disclosed to HR for review.

Unrelated personal projects, lawful activities and nonconflicting freelance work are not subject to a blanket disclosure requirement under this policy. Any restriction must be consistent with the contract and Bangladesh law.

**Example:** Creating an unrelated personal illustration after work is not automatically a conflict. Taking private paid work from a Blinto client on the same account without authorization is a potential conflict.

## Recognizing a Conflict of Interest

A conflict can exist where a personal, financial, family or outside business interest might affect—or reasonably appear to affect—professional judgment. Examples include:

- recommending a supplier in which the employee has a personal financial interest;
- influencing hiring, evaluation, supervision or purchasing decisions involving a relative, partner or close friend;
- receiving personal benefits from vendors or clients because of a Blinto business decision;
- operating a competing business while accessing Blinto client or pricing information;
- privately accepting payment from Blinto clients without necessary approval; or
- using company opportunities or confidential client information for personal gain.

Having a relationship or outside interest does not by itself establish misconduct. Employees should disclose potential conflicts early so they can be assessed fairly.

## Gifts, Hospitality, and Benefits

**All client or vendor gifts offered in connection with work must be disclosed to HR.** Employees may not solicit or accept cash gifts, personal kickbacks, secret commissions, or benefits intended to influence work decisions.

Modest promotional gifts may be accepted after disclosure where HR has no objection and the gift does not create an actual or perceived conflict. Client or vendor meals and hospitality require appropriate approval when related to company business. Gifts that could influence a business decision must be declined.

**Example:** A modest promotional notebook disclosed to HR may be acceptable; cash offered by a vendor for selection is not.

## Disclosing and Resolving a Conflict

Employees must inform **HR as soon as a potential conflict becomes known**. They may also inform their Department Head if that person is not part of the conflict. If HR or the Department Head is involved, the employee may approach an uninvolved senior leader or the Chief of Staff.

HR reviews the situation confidentially as far as practical, considers the actual risk, and communicates any outcome or restriction. While a material conflict is under review, the affected employee should avoid making or influencing the related decision.

Possible responses include independent review, reassignment of a particular decision, limited access, separation of reporting relationships, declining the proposed engagement, or other proportionate safeguards.

**Chief of Staff final approval is required for any material conflict-of-interest exception** recommended after HR review. No exception can permit bribery, unlawful disclosure, or breach of binding client obligations.

## Work Product, Portfolio Use, and Intellectual Property

Work created for Blinto or a client as part of assigned duties must be saved in the approved company-controlled location. Rights to source code, designs, documents and other deliverables are determined by applicable copyright and other law, the employment or engagement agreement, and relevant client contracts.

Do not use confidential client code, designs, documents or project files in personal work; publish unpublished client materials in a portfolio; or reuse protected company opportunities without permission. Obtain authorization before publicly showing confidential work samples. Open-source and third-party materials must be used in accordance with applicable licenses.

Pre-existing personal creations remain governed by their actual ownership and agreements; working at Blinto does not automatically transfer every unrelated personal creation to Blinto.

**Example:** An employee cannot publish unreleased client website designs in a public portfolio without permission. An unrelated illustration created before joining is not automatically company property.

## Privacy, Reporting, and Fair Review

HR should collect and use only the information reasonably required to assess a conflict. Family relationships, financial interests, personal records and disclosure details must be restricted to authorized people with a legitimate need. The [Employee Data Privacy & Records Management Policy](./employee-data-privacy-and-records-management-policy.md), once approved, governs related personal-data handling.

Good-faith disclosure or asking for advice should not by itself lead to adverse treatment. Alleged concealment, confidentiality breaches, bribery, or other misconduct will be considered fairly under the applicable [Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy](./corrective-action-and-performance-improvement-policy.md) and legal requirements, including an opportunity to respond.

## Responsibilities

**Employees, interns, and authorized collaborators:** Protect confidential data; disclose work-related gifts and material conflicts; ask for approval when needed; and report suspected breaches.

**Department Heads:** Reinforce these obligations, avoid decisions involving personal conflicts, and work with HR on appropriate safeguards.

**People Operations / HR:** Receive and assess disclosures, safeguard records, explain restrictions and handle employee-policy concerns.

**Chief of Staff:** Decide material conflict-of-interest exceptions after HR review.

**Authorized system and data owners:** Apply security and access controls under existing approved SOPs.

## Implementation Status

This is a **draft policy**, not an approved or published company rule. Blinto has identified that employees are unclear about how to disclose conflicts. The policy proposes an HR reporting route, review safeguards, clear gift-disclosure expectations, and Chief of Staff approval for material exceptions.

It does **not** claim that a dedicated conflict register, automated HRMS form, surveillance capability or other new workflow currently exists. HR may retain appropriate confidential records in an approved controlled location until a separate system is established.

## Bangladesh Legal Context

- **Contract Act, 1872, section 27:** Broad restrictions on the exercise of a lawful profession, trade or business may be void; confidentiality and conflict rules should not be misrepresented as an unrestricted non-compete.
- **Copyright Act, 2023, section 15:** First ownership of work varies according to the nature of the work, employment or apprenticeship circumstances, and applicable agreements. Client-specific intellectual property terms must also be checked.
- **Personal Data Protection Act, 2026 (Act No. 63 of 2026):** Applicable personal-data protections and lawful-processing requirements apply to employee records and conflict disclosures.

Applicable law, lawful reporting rights and enforceable agreements prevail over this draft.
