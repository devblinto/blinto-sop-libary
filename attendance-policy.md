# Attendance Policy

## Purpose

Define Blinto's expectations for attendance, punctuality, late arrival, attendance communication, required meetings, and attendance records.

This policy explains when to arrive, how to record attendance, what happens after repeated late arrivals, and how to correct attendance mistakes.

The normal workday is defined in [Office Hours Policy](./office-hours-policy.md). Work beyond normal hours is covered by [Overtime Policy](./overtime-policy.md).

## Scope

This policy applies to all Blinto employees according to their approved working schedules.

Employees with approved remote work, flexible schedules, leave, or other arrangements follow the attendance requirements that apply to those arrangements. Interns and other team members follow their approved engagement terms and schedules.

## Attendance Expectations

Team members are expected to:

- attend work according to their approved schedule;
- normally aim to arrive by **8:30 AM** under the standard office schedule;
- check in and check out accurately using the approved attendance method;
- be ready for required meetings and client commitments on time;
- return from scheduled breaks on time;
- tell their Team/Department Lead or People Operations about a foreseeable absence or significant delay as early as reasonably possible; and
- keep their lead informed if an attendance issue may affect delivery or team coverage.

Approved leave, authorized remote work, emergencies, and other valid exceptions must be considered before attendance is treated as unexcused.

## Punctuality

Under the standard office schedule:

- **Office start:** 8:30 AM
- **Normal arrival window:** 8:30–8:45 AM
- **Late arrival:** Any check-in **after 8:45 AM**, unless an approved exception applies

The 8:45 AM late threshold does not change the standard office start time of 8:30 AM.

**Example:** A check-in at 8:40 AM is within the arrival window. A check-in at 8:50 AM is late, but may use an automatic late allowance if eligible.

## Automatic Late Allowance

Each employee may use **up to 2 automatic late-arrival allowances per calendar month**.

A late arrival qualifies for an allowance only when:

1. the employee checks in **after 8:45 AM and no later than 9:30 AM**;
2. an unused monthly allowance is available; and
3. there is no separate approved schedule exception covering that arrival.

The **9:30 AM** cutoff is **one hour after the standard 8:30 AM office start**. It is not one hour after the 8:45 AM late threshold.

The allowance helps with occasional unavoidable delays. It is **not** permission to routinely start work late.

- Unused allowances do not carry over to another month.
- An approved schedule exception does not use an automatic allowance.
- A check-in **after 9:30 AM** cannot use an automatic allowance, even if the employee has not used both allowances.
- A significant delay should still be communicated as early as reasonably possible.
- An allowance does not automatically excuse a missed meeting or client commitment.

**Example:** An employee arrives at 9:10 AM on one day and 9:20 AM on another day in the same month. If both arrivals are otherwise eligible, the two monthly allowances are used. A later unexcused late arrival follows the escalation process.

## Automated Monthly Late-Arrival Escalation

HRMS must record unexcused late-arrival events and apply the following monthly sequence. Approved leave, schedule exceptions, emergencies, and corrected attendance records must be excluded when appropriate.

| Late arrival in the calendar month | HRMS treatment |
| :-- | :-- |
| **Late #1** | Automatic late allowance, **if eligible**; otherwise flag for People Operations review |
| **Late #2** | Automatic late allowance, **if eligible**; otherwise flag for People Operations review |
| **Late #3** | Automatic reminder to the employee |
| **Late #4** | Automatic coaching/escalation notice to the employee, Team/Department Lead, and People Operations |
| **Late #5** | Generate a **pending formal attendance warning** for People Operations verification and the required employee-response process |
| **Late #6 and onward** | Automatic escalation to People Operations and the responsible manager for further review under the Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy |

### Late Arrivals After 9:30 AM

An unexcused check-in after 9:30 AM is **not** a qualifying automatic allowance, even if it is the employee's first or second late-arrival event of the month.

HRMS must record the event and flag it for People Operations review. People Operations will check the facts, any valid exceptions, and the appropriate corrective response. The system must not label the event as an approved allowance.

**Example:** An employee's first late arrival of the month is at 9:40 AM. This counts as a late-arrival event but does **not** use an automatic allowance. People Operations reviews it. A later qualifying arrival may still use an unused allowance, subject to the monthly event sequence and HRMS rules. The precise HRMS treatment of this mixed sequence must be tested before release.

### Monthly Reset and History

The monthly event counter resets at the beginning of each calendar month. HRMS must keep the history of attendance events, approved corrections, reminders, warnings, and decisions so recurring patterns can be reviewed fairly.

Resetting the monthly counter does not erase an employee's attendance history or an existing formal decision.

## Formal Warning Review

HRMS may automatically identify a case that reaches the formal-warning stage, but the warning **must not become final solely because the system generated it**.

Before finalizing a formal warning, People Operations must:

1. check that the attendance records are accurate;
2. verify relevant approved leave, schedule changes, emergencies, and corrections;
3. tell the employee which attendance events are being reviewed;
4. give the employee a reasonable opportunity to explain or dispute the facts;
5. follow the applicable employment contract, approved corrective-action process, and any legally required disciplinary procedure; and
6. document the outcome and authorized decision-maker.

People Operations may correct or withdraw a proposed warning when the records are wrong or a valid exception applies.

A formal disciplinary penalty must follow applicable law and the [Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy](./corrective-action-and-performance-improvement-policy.md). This attendance sequence does not authorize automatic termination, wage deductions, or other legal penalties.

## Attendance Check-In and Check-Out

Employees must use Blinto HRMS or another approved method to record attendance accurately.

Employees must not check in or out for another person or deliberately submit false attendance information.

A missing punch does not, by itself, prove that an employee was absent or engaged in misconduct.

## Missing Check-In or Check-Out

When an employee forgets a check-in or check-out, or a genuine system problem occurs:

1. The employee submits an **attendance correction request in HRMS** with the date, proposed time, and reason.
2. **People Operations checks the request with the relevant Team/Department Lead.**
3. **People Operations approves or rejects** the request based on available information.
4. HRMS keeps an **audit trail** showing the original record, request, reviewer, decision, and change date.

Employees should request corrections as soon as reasonably possible. If HRMS is unavailable, they should contact People Operations through an approved communication channel so the issue can be recorded and reviewed.

A disputed record should be reviewed before it is relied on for a formal warning or another formal action.

## Office and Remote Attendance Verification

### Office Attendance

Employees scheduled to work in the office should check in using the approved office attendance method. Blinto may use **approved office Wi-Fi or network verification** to help confirm office-based check-ins.

A network problem is not automatically evidence of a policy violation. Employees may report technical issues for review.

### Remote Attendance

Employees need an **approved remote-work arrangement or location exception** before using remote attendance, except where a genuine emergency or other valid circumstance requires later review.

Remote employees must follow their approved schedule and attendance requirements.

### Suspicious or Disputed Records

People Operations may individually review a record when a check-in appears inconsistent with the employee's approved work location, the office network cannot be verified, or other reasonable grounds for concern exist.

The employee must have an opportunity to explain technical problems, approved remote work, emergencies, or other relevant facts before a conclusion is reached.

Attendance verification should use only the information reasonably needed for attendance administration and follow applicable privacy and security rules.

## Daily Stand-Up and Meeting Punctuality

Under the standard office schedule, the daily stand-up runs from **9:00–9:15 AM**.

Stand-up punctuality and arrival punctuality are **tracked separately**.

Employees should join required meetings on time, be prepared, and tell their lead as early as reasonably possible if they cannot attend.

An automatic late-arrival allowance does not automatically excuse a missed stand-up. However, approved exceptions and the employee's circumstances must be considered. A lead may ask an excused employee to provide the update through an approved alternative method.

The same incident should not trigger duplicate or disproportionate corrective action.

## Absence and Delay Communication

Employees should inform their Team/Department Lead or People Operations about a known absence or significant delay as early as reasonably possible using an approved communication method.

If an emergency prevents advance notice, they should communicate as soon as reasonably possible afterward.

Sending a message is not, by itself, approval for leave or an ongoing schedule change. Applicable leave and schedule-approval procedures still apply.

## Attendance Records

People Operations maintains or oversees the official attendance record and process.

Attendance records should distinguish, where relevant, between:

- normal attendance;
- qualifying automatic late allowance;
- late arrival outside the allowance;
- approved leave;
- approved schedule or location exception;
- approved remote work;
- missing or disputed check-in/check-out;
- early departure;
- absence; and
- other relevant attendance statuses.

HRMS must retain correction decisions, notifications, formal-warning outcomes, and historical records appropriately.

Required-meeting attendance may be recorded separately when there is an approved operational need.

Overtime records are managed under [Overtime Policy](./overtime-policy.md) rather than being treated as ordinary attendance records.

## Recurring Attendance Issues

Repeated lateness, unauthorized absence, missed required meetings, early departure, or failure to communicate attendance problems should be reviewed as a pattern rather than ignored indefinitely.

The responsible lead and People Operations should:

1. check the facts and any approved exceptions;
2. identify the underlying problem;
3. explain the expected standard;
4. provide reasonable coaching or support;
5. document material or repeated issues where necessary; and
6. follow the applicable corrective-action process if concerns continue.

The aim is to improve attendance and reliability, not impose unnecessary punishment.

The monthly HRMS sequence governs late-arrival administration, subject to the verification and response safeguards above. Other attendance concerns follow the proportionate, case-based framework in [Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy](./corrective-action-and-performance-improvement-policy.md).

## Recognition

Blinto may recognize strong attendance and punctuality through the company's Rewards & Recognition program.

The details of monthly attendance awards, eligibility, prizes, and award selection belong in the canonical Blinto Benefits hub rather than this policy.

Approved leave and legally protected absence must be considered fairly in award eligibility rules.

## Responsibilities

**Team Members** are responsible for following their approved schedules, recording attendance accurately, communicating attendance problems, requesting corrections, and attending required meetings unless excused.

**Team/Department Leads** reinforce attendance and meeting expectations, assess delivery impact, verify correction requests when contacted, coach team members, and escalate recurring concerns appropriately.

**People Operations** oversees attendance administration, approved exceptions, records, corrections, pending-warning review, policy consistency, and HRMS requirements.

**Leaders and Managers** are expected to follow their applicable attendance rules and model the punctuality standards they ask others to meet.

## Exceptions & Escalation

Contact People Operations when:

- a recurring schedule accommodation is needed;
- remote work or a change in approved work location is requested;
- a technical check-in problem occurs;
- an attendance record is disputed;
- an emergency affects attendance;
- a late arrival falls outside the automatic allowance;
- repeated absence, missed meetings, or lateness becomes a concern;
- a formal attendance warning is being considered; or
- the correct treatment is unclear.

People Operations will consider the facts, valid exceptions, applicable employment terms, and any required legal process before determining the next step.

## Policy Administration

People Operations maintains this policy and coordinates future proposed revisions.

Any changes to monthly allowances, arrival thresholds, escalation rules, or formal disciplinary treatment require the usual Blinto policy review and approval process.

Approved changes must be communicated to employees with an effective date before implementation.
