# Overtime Policy

## Purpose

Explain which Blinto employees may receive overtime payment, the **three exceptional situations** in which overtime may be approved, how actual work is verified, and how it is submitted to HR and Finance.

Blinto plans work so that normal responsibilities are completed during approved working hours. Overtime is an exception, not an alternative to reasonable planning, productivity, staffing, or delivery management.


## Eligibility and Role Classification

**Blinto's company-policy overtime program is intended only for operational-level employees.** Department Heads, employees genuinely performing managerial or supervisory responsibilities, and executive or leadership roles are **not eligible for discretionary company-policy overtime payments** under this draft.

For eligibility and payment questions, HR verifies the person's actual legal classification with Finance and, when necessary, Bangladesh legal counsel before making a final decision. This policy does not create overtime pay for management roles where no legal or contractual entitlement exists.

## Normal Hours and What Counts as Overtime

Blinto's normal office hours are **8:30 AM–5:00 PM**, as set by [Office Hours Policy](./office-hours-policy.md). The normal lunch and prayer break is **1:00–2:15 PM**. Applicable approved schedule exceptions and weekly rest arrangements must also be respected.

For the company program, overtime is **actual additional work assigned or authorized for an eligible employee in one of the three situations below**, verified by work evidence and actual start/end times. Simply staying at the office, leaving HRMS checked in, completing ordinary pending tasks late, or voluntarily working beyond the schedule does not automatically create an approved company-policy overtime payment.

Actual hours worked must still be accurately recorded, including where statutory overtime or rest-day obligations may apply. An administrative approval requirement cannot extinguish a legally required payment.

## The Only Three Overtime Scenarios

### Scenario 1 — Necessary Work to Prevent a Justifiable Project-Delivery Deadline Miss

Overtime may be considered when a genuine project deadline is at risk **for reasonable, demonstrable reasons outside avoidable delay during normal working hours**. Examples may include an unanticipated client dependency, material scope change, or critical external blocker that could not reasonably be resolved within the normal schedule.

- The responsible Department Head or Project Delivery Head documents the deadline, blocker, impact, operational alternatives, employees needed, and estimated additional hours.
- **The CEO is the approving authority for overtime-payment eligibility in this scenario.** The CEO's authorization is required before planned overtime where practical.
- Following the work, the Department Head verifies actual hours and delivery outcome; HR validates the submission and Finance calculates any payable amount.
- A deadline missed because of routine procrastination, avoidable underperformance, or work delayed during normal hours is **not** a basis for discretionary overtime approval.

### Scenario 2 — Urgent Client Work or Incident After Working Hours

Overtime may be considered for urgent client requests, production incidents, critical bugs, outages, or other time-sensitive client issues that genuinely require action after normal hours.

- **The Department Head assigns the task.** The **Project Delivery Head and the relevant Department Head** authorize the business need and verify the actual overtime work and hours, according to their responsibilities. Where one person holds both responsibilities, HR documents that fact rather than inventing an additional sign-off.
- The Department Head provides HR with the task details, actual hours, outcome, and evidence.
- HR checks eligibility, records, and consistency. **For disputed, unusual, or insufficiently verified cases, HR may refer the matter to the CEO for confirmation or further approval.** CEO review is not a universal prerequisite for every routine Scenario 2 request.
- Finance calculates payment after the verification and authorized payroll process.

### Scenario 3 — Urgent Client Issue During a Government Public Holiday

When an urgent client issue arises during a **government-declared public holiday observed by Blinto**—including a long sequence of official public holidays—the Department Head may assign necessary work to an eligible operational employee when the issue cannot reasonably wait until the office reopens.

- The Department Head confirms the urgent client requirement, assigns the task, and verifies the actual time worked and completed outcome.
- The Department Head informs HR of the assignment and provides the supporting client/project evidence and verified working hours.
- The employee submits the work details and evidence through the HR-designated Google Form after completing the work.
- HR verifies the public-holiday date, employee eligibility, actual hours and approval record; Finance calculates the applicable payment and any statutory holiday-work entitlement.
- Any legally required compensatory holiday, rest or holiday-work benefit is handled under applicable Bangladesh law and the approved holiday/leave arrangements. This scenario concerns **government public holidays**, not an employee's personal, medical or other approved leave.

These three situations are the exhaustive **company discretionary overtime-approval categories** under this draft. They do not narrow statutory rights to payment or working-hour protection when covered work actually occurs in other circumstances.

## Requests, Evidence and Interim Google Form Process

**The current HRMS cannot accept the necessary overtime request and supporting-document workflow.** Until Blinto approves an appropriate HRMS feature or replacement process, employees must submit overtime claims **through a Google Form designated by HR**. The policy does not assume that the form, routing automation, or digital approvals already exist; HR must communicate the actual form link and operating instructions before launch.

The employee's Google Form submission should identify:

- employee name/ID, department and responsible Department Head;
- the applicable scenario (1, 2, or 3);
- work date, normal scheduled hours, actual overtime start and end times, and breaks;
- client, project, incident or task reference, where applicable;
- why extra work was necessary and why it could not wait until normal hours;
- **supporting documents** showing assigned client work, instructions, ClickUp/project records, incident messages or meeting references, and the **work outcome/deliverable**;
- the relevant Department Head / Project Delivery Head confirmation and CEO decision where Scenario 1 requires it; and
- any exception explaining why the request could not be documented before urgent work began.

Employee submissions go to **HR/People Operations for verification**, not to a fictional HRMS overtime feature. HR retains access-controlled evidence and a traceable approval record and sends verified payroll information to Finance. An HRMS attendance timestamp, ClickUp completion time, or meeting invitation is not by itself proof of the exact duration of work.

### Approval and verification sequence

1. **Before planned work:** Department Head identifies the scenario and assigns the work. Scenario 1 also requires the CEO's approval of overtime-payment eligibility; Scenario 2 requires Project Delivery Head and Department Head authorization; Scenario 3 requires Department Head authorization with HR verification of the official public-holiday status.
2. **For a genuine emergency:** The responsible Department Head may arrange the minimum necessary protective/client work immediately and inform HR as soon as reasonably practicable. Required case-specific approval and verification follow promptly.
3. **Employee submits Google Form:** Provide actual time, supporting documents, client/project work and outcome as soon as reasonably practical after completion.
4. **Department/Project Delivery review:** Confirm instructions, necessity, output and actual hours; distinguish performed work from idle, travel or rest time as legally appropriate.
5. **HR verifies:** Check employee classification, scenario-specific approvals, supporting evidence, breaks, applicable legal entitlements and any holiday/attendance adjustments. HR may refer exceptional Scenario 2 cases to the CEO.
6. **Finance calculates and processes:** Apply the lawful wage basis and rate, confirm payroll approval, and communicate verified hours and payment treatment to the employee.

Employees may submit a good-faith claim for time actually worked even when an approval or form submission was delayed or omitted; HR and Finance must review the facts and any statutory or contractual entitlement instead of automatically refusing payment. False documentation or deliberate inflation of hours may be addressed through a fair process under the Corrective Action Policy.

## Compensation and Working-Time Safeguards

Blinto's existing Overtime Policy uses a **2× overtime payment standard**, subject to the applicable legal definition and wage basis. For workers covered by section 108 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, qualifying statutory overtime is payable at **twice the ordinary rate of basic wages plus applicable dearness, ad hoc, or interim allowances**. The applicable Labour Rules calculation must be used; this is not automatically twice the employee's total gross salary.

Finance calculates and verifies payments; HR confirms eligibility, actual work evidence, and case-specific approval. Company procedures must not undermine statutory working-hour limits, weekly holidays/rest, meal breaks, or legally protected leave. Any work done outside these three approved scenarios is still reviewed for actual statutory or contractual pay obligations where relevant.

The approved late-arrival rules and attendance exceptions remain governed by [Attendance Policy](./attendance-policy.md); overtime cannot be used to erase lateness or create an ordinary alternative work schedule. Overtime is not inferred solely from HRMS check-in/out records.

## Responsibilities

**Operational employees:** Obtain assignment or emergency authorization, log actual hours and breaks, provide reliable documents and work outcomes through the HR-designated Google Form, and report missing pay or disputed records to HR.

**Department Heads:** Avoid unnecessary overtime, assign necessary tasks, verify business needs and actual work, keep HR informed, and apply the scenario-specific approval path. Department Heads themselves are outside the proposed discretionary overtime-payment program when their principal role is managerial/supervisory.

**Project Delivery Head:** Authorize and verify Scenario 2 client urgency alongside the Department Head, including time spent and outcome.

**CEO:** Approve Scenario 1 overtime-payment eligibility; consider exceptional Scenario 2 cases referred by HR; receive significant recurring overtime or classification risks as needed.

**People Operations/HR:** Operate the interim Google Form process, verify classification, scenario, approvals, hours and evidence, maintain restricted records, review holiday/attendance impacts, and send Finance accurate payable information. HR does not replace the CEO's Scenario 1 approval or the Project Delivery/Department Head's Scenario 2 operational verification.

**Finance:** Calculate legally payable overtime and process it through the approved payroll controls, maintain payment evidence, and cooperate with HR on disputes.

## Questions, Corrections and Disputes

Employees may ask HR to review missing hours, disputed approvals, incorrect overtime calculations, or work performed in an emergency. HR consults the Department Head, Project Delivery Head, Finance and CEO where appropriate, documents the outcome, and corrects payroll records when justified. Reporting a genuine concern in good faith is not misconduct.
