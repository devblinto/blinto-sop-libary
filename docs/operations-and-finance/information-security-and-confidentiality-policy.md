# Information Security & Confidentiality Policy

## Purpose

Protect Blinto's company information, client information, personal data and business systems from unauthorized access, disclosure, alteration, loss and misuse. Everyone who handles this information is responsible for protecting it, whether working in the office, remotely or at a client location.

This policy provides common information-security expectations. The approved Credential Policy and Access Provisioning SOP remain authoritative for credential management, access approvals, provisioning and revocation. Follow the relevant detailed policies for implementation; this policy does not replace them or establish a competing approval process.

## Scope

This policy applies to confirmed and probationary employees, interns, Department Heads, team leads and management, as well as authorized freelancers, contractors, vendors and other individuals who use Blinto-managed systems or handle Blinto or client information. External users must follow the applicable engagement terms, confidentiality obligations and authorized access restrictions.

## Employee Guidance

### 1. Information we protect

Protect information in every form, including documents, source code, messages, recordings, screenshots, printed materials and verbal conversations. Examples include:

- Client files, project deliverables, customer and end-user information.
- Company source code, intellectual property, internal processes, business plans and strategies.
- Employee personal information, HR records, salaries, payroll and financial records.
- Contracts, commercial agreements, pricing and confidential communications or meeting records.
- Passwords, API keys, recovery codes, authentication tokens and other secrets.

Information remains protected even if it is copied, downloaded, shared for a project or stored outside the office with authorization.

### 2. Understand the sensitivity of information

Use the following practical categories when deciding how to handle information. No new classification software is required.

| Category | Examples | Handling |
|---|---|---|
| Public | Published company announcements, approved marketing materials | Share externally only when authorized for publication or distribution. |
| Internal | Routine work instructions, internal schedules and nonpublic operational updates | Limit to appropriate company personnel and authorized collaborators. |
| Confidential | Client work, contracts, unpublished source code, nonpublic business information | Share only with authorized people who need the information for their work; use approved restricted storage. |
| Highly sensitive | Credentials, privileged access details, payroll, personal identification and medical records, sensitive client or payment data | Apply additional restrictions, use the designated secure location and limit access to specifically authorized people. Credentials must follow the Credential Policy. |

A client's classification and security requirements may impose stricter controls. Follow them where applicable. If unsure, treat information as restricted and ask the Department Head or responsible information owner before sharing. Consult HR about employee information and authorized system administrators about technical handling.

### 3. Store and share information safely

- Use approved company Google Workspace accounts and authorized storage locations for company documents. Use approved ClickUp workspaces for relevant work records, with permissions appropriate to the content.
- Follow the Credential Policy for storing or sharing passwords, tokens, API keys and other secrets. Do not put them in ordinary documents, email, ClickUp tasks or Telegram messages.
- Use Telegram for routine coordination, not sensitive employee records, financial details, credentials or confidential files that require controlled access.
- Do not transfer company or client information to personal email, personal cloud storage or unauthorized apps without appropriate authorization and security review.
- Do not create public or unrestricted links for confidential information. Check recipients and permissions before sharing, including when forwarding, attaching files or sending meeting recordings.
- Share externally only for an authorized business purpose with the correct recipient, using approved channels and applicable confidentiality arrangements. Share only the information necessary for that purpose.
- Use client-approved storage and systems for client information when required. Do not copy client data into another workspace simply for convenience.
- Remove unnecessary sharing access when a task, project or responsibility changes, following the Access Provisioning SOP and the responsible system owner's instructions.

For example, a project update may be suitable for ClickUp or routine Telegram coordination, but a client's production credentials belong in the approved credential-management process, not the project task.

### 4. Protect accounts and access

Follow the approved Credential Policy and Access Provisioning SOP. In particular:

- Request access only for legitimate assigned work. Use the approved access request and authorization process; do not authorize your own sensitive access.
- Use individual authorized accounts wherever possible, with the minimum permissions needed for the role. Do not use another person's identity or share credentials contrary to the Credential Policy.
- Use multi-factor authentication as required by the existing security rules and applicable client requirements. Never approve unexpected authentication requests or disclose verification codes to an unauthorized person.
- Inform your Department Head or system owner when access is no longer needed. Department Heads review team permissions under the existing access-review process; authorized administrators implement approved changes.
- Do not bypass restrictions, attempt unauthorized access, disable security controls or test company or client systems without explicit authorization.

### 5. Protect devices and remote work

Follow the Company Asset Issuance, Use & Return Policy, Acceptable Use of Company Technology Policy and Remote Work & Work From Home Policy.

Keep devices secured with approved passwords or screen locks, lock the screen when unattended, and apply approved security and software updates. Do not install unauthorized applications or browser extensions. Report lost or stolen devices immediately.

When working outside the office, protect screens, conversations and files from unauthorized viewing. Use authorized devices, accounts and connections, follow client restrictions, and avoid storing sensitive data in unapproved locations. Personal devices may be used for company work only when authorized and subject to applicable security requirements. Do not allow family members or others to use company work devices or accounts.

### 6. Use AI tools and external services responsibly

Follow the Responsible Use of AI Tools Policy and client-specific restrictions. Do not submit credentials, sensitive personal information, confidential company material or client data to an unauthorized AI service or other external service. Obtain the required authorization before using a service that processes protected information. An approved subscription alone does not authorize every kind of data disclosure.

### 7. Report information security concerns immediately

Report suspected incidents promptly, even if you are not certain that a breach occurred. Examples include phishing messages, unexpected authentication requests, suspected account compromise, accidental sharing, missing devices, unusual access, lost files and disclosure to the wrong recipient.

Contact the responsible system owner or authorized system administrator for technical incidents, and inform your Department Head when work or client information may be affected. Inform HR when employee information is involved, and Finance when financial or payment information is involved. If the responsible contact is unclear or the incident is significant, contact the Chief of Staff directly. Do not wait for a routine meeting or formal approval before reporting an urgent concern.

Explain what happened, when it happened, the affected system or information, and any action already taken. Use a restricted channel appropriate to the sensitivity of the information. Do not include passwords or unnecessary personal data in the report.

Stop the unsafe activity where possible. Do not open suspicious attachments, approve unexpected login requests, delete potential evidence, independently investigate beyond your authorization or make unapproved technical changes. Follow authorized instructions to contain the incident. Preserve relevant messages, logs and other evidence securely.

Do not independently notify clients, the public or media about a security incident on Blinto's behalf. Authorized personnel coordinate necessary external communications. This does not restrict lawful reporting, complaints, cooperation with competent authorities or protected disclosures.

Good-faith reporting is encouraged and is not, by itself, grounds for disciplinary action.

## Responsibilities and Internal Response Procedure

### Responsibilities

**Employees and authorized external users:** Follow this policy, protect information, report concerns promptly and cooperate with authorized response actions.

**Department Heads and team leads:** Reinforce security expectations, confirm legitimate business access needs, review team access under the approved Access Provisioning SOP, communicate applicable client requirements and coordinate team-level response and continuity.

**Authorized system administrators and responsible system owners:** Implement authorized access and technical changes; support investigation, containment, evidence preservation and recovery within their responsibilities. They must not independently grant unauthorized access or make unrelated changes.

**People Operations (HR):** Coordinate security and confidentiality orientation, protect employee records, manage employee-data incident matters and coordinate any fair employee-related review under the applicable policy.

**Finance:** Coordinate incidents involving payroll, financial or payment data, including verification of suspicious payment instructions and authorized steps to protect affected financial transactions.

**Chief of Staff:** Oversee significant information security incidents, coordinate responsible functions and authorized external response, and escalate serious incidents or material risks to the CEO.

**CEO:** Receive escalation of serious incidents or material business risks and exercise existing management authority for major decisions. Existing employment decision approvals remain unchanged.

### Incident response

1. **Receive and assess.** The recipient promptly directs the concern to the responsible system owner and relevant Department Head, HR or Finance. Identify what information or systems may be affected, whether access is ongoing, and the urgency. Escalate significant or potentially material incidents to the Chief of Staff without waiting for certainty.
2. **Contain and protect.** Authorized administrators or system owners take proportionate protective action under existing access and security procedures, such as restricting affected access, revoking exposed sessions or removing an unauthorized sharing link. Preserve evidence before destructive changes where feasible. Urgent protective access restrictions do not constitute a disciplinary finding.
3. **Investigate and coordinate.** Establish the known facts, affected data, recipients, potential impact and necessary corrective steps. Restrict incident information to those who need it. HR handles employee-data and employee-process matters; Finance handles financial matters; Department Heads coordinate client and work impacts. Do not assume that an incident occurred merely because a concern was reported.
4. **Assess notification obligations.** The Chief of Staff coordinates with responsible functions and appropriate legal or specialist advisers to determine applicable regulatory, data-subject, client and contractual reporting requirements, recipients and deadlines. Required notifications must not be delayed by internal approval. Only authorized personnel communicate on behalf of Blinto; retain a record of the decision and communications.
5. **Recover and close.** Authorized owners restore safe access and operations, correct affected permissions or records, verify that identified risks have been addressed and document the outcome and responsible follow-up actions. Escalate unresolved risks to the Chief of Staff and serious matters to the CEO.

Maintain a restricted record of material incidents, decisions, actions, relevant evidence and follow-up. Use authorized existing storage or a suitable restricted record if the existing system lacks the required functionality. Do not create an unrestricted incident log or assume an automated detection, ticketing or backup platform exists.

## Information Retention, Personal Data and Confidentiality

Collect, use and disclose personal data only for a legitimate specified purpose and on an applicable lawful basis. Obtain valid consent where required; do not assume employment or a signed confidentiality agreement authorizes every use of personal information. Limit collection and access to what is needed, keep information accurate, and apply appropriate security and confidentiality safeguards. Refer employee-data access, correction or other rights requests to HR; refer other personal-data requests to the responsible information owner for lawful handling.

Before transferring protected data to an external service or another country, obtain the applicable authorization and verify relevant legal, client and contractual restrictions. Use appropriate safeguards for vendors and processors handling personal information; management and the responsible information owner must verify applicable obligations before disclosure.

Retain records according to applicable law, contractual requirements and the relevant records-management policy. Dispose of or delete information securely when authorized and no longer required. Do not destroy information subject to an investigation, legal preservation requirement or other applicable retention obligation.

Confidentiality continues after employment or an engagement ends to the extent required by law and valid contractual obligations. It does not prevent lawful reporting, exercising statutory rights or disclosures required by law.

## Onboarding, Role Changes and Offboarding

People Operations introduces security and confidentiality expectations during onboarding. Department Heads explain role-specific and client-specific requirements, approved systems, permitted information handling and reporting contacts. Provide guidance when relevant procedures change.

When responsibilities change, review access and transfer information ownership through the approved Access Provisioning SOP. On departure, follow Employee Offboarding for handover, return of assets and company information, transfer of ownership and timely access revocation. Where shared credentials were exposed, authorized owners follow the existing credential procedure to change or invalidate them. Do not impose blanket rotation where individual revocation is sufficient under approved procedures.

In a high-risk departure, immediate protective action may be necessary while the formal employment decision follows the existing approval and legal process. Required final wages and other statutory dues must not be withheld solely because an information-security or asset-return matter remains unresolved.

## Compliance and Fair Review

Intentional misuse, unauthorized disclosure or other proven violations may lead to corrective action under the applicable Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy and lawful employment procedures. Assess mistakes according to their actual circumstances, impact, intent, prior guidance and severity. Allow the individual an appropriate opportunity to respond; comply with applicable inquiry, notice and decision requirements before formal sanctions.

This policy does not introduce employee surveillance, monitoring software, automatic penalties or wage deductions. Security investigations and access restrictions must be authorized, proportionate and lawful. External users are subject to the applicable contract and law rather than automatically to employee disciplinary procedures.

Apply the Personal Data Protection Act, 2026, the Cyber Security Act, 2026 as amended, applicable Bangladesh labour law and relevant contractual requirements when handling information and incidents. Where obligations conflict or are uncertain, escalate to the Chief of Staff and appropriate legal advisers before proceeding, without delaying urgent lawful protective action or mandatory reporting.
