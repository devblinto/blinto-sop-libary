# Disciplinary Investigation & Employee Response SOP

## Purpose

Explain how Blinto handles serious or disputed workplace allegations fairly, protects everyone involved, examines reliable evidence, allows the employee to respond, and makes appropriately authorized decisions.

**This SOP governs investigations and employee response.** The existing [Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy](./corrective-action-and-performance-improvement-policy.md) governs ordinary coaching, warnings and improvement plans. The [Employee Offboarding](./employee-offboarding.md) SOP governs operational separation and currently retains the approval requirements for involuntary termination.

This document contains an **employee-facing explanation** followed by a **separately identified internal HR process**. It does not create a reporting form, investigation template, or automatic disciplinary penalty.

# Part A — What Employees Should Know

## Who Is Covered and When This SOP Applies

The process applies to employees, including probationary employees and paid interns where relevant, when a serious or disputed allegation requires formal examination. For consultants and other nonemployee collaborators, HR and the engagement owner should use the applicable agreement and lawful process, while following appropriate confidentiality and fairness standards.

An investigation may be appropriate for alleged serious misconduct, repeated significant rule violations, harassment, bullying, discrimination, misuse of company or client information, suspected theft or dishonesty, serious attendance or behavioral concerns, or a material security/confidentiality incident.

**A mistake, an isolated minor lapse, a weak performance result, or an ordinary coaching conversation does not automatically require a disciplinary investigation.** HR first decides which process is proportionate to the concern.

## Reporting a Concern

An employee may tell **HR** about an allegation or concern. Department Heads should report material issues to HR rather than privately decide serious disciplinary cases. If HR or the employee's manager is involved, the employee may report directly to the **Chief of Staff**.

Written reports are preferred, but HR will also accept a verbal report and record the relevant facts. Employees may raise concerns privately. Anonymous reports may be assessed where there is enough information to review the allegation fairly; an anonymous assertion alone is not proof of misconduct.

Blinto will restrict the sharing of sensitive information to those who genuinely need it. Absolute secrecy cannot be promised when disclosure is necessary to investigate, allow a response, protect people, or comply with law. Good-faith reporting and participation must not lead to retaliation.

## How an Allegation Will Be Handled

HR may first conduct **preliminary fact-finding** to establish what was reported, whether there is an immediate safety or security concern, and whether the matter can be resolved through guidance, coaching, or another existing process.

If a formal statutory misconduct proceeding is required for a worker covered by the Bangladesh Labour Act, the employee will receive a **written charge and a copy of the allegations**, with **at least seven days to explain**. The employee must have an opportunity to be heard through the legally prescribed process. A short preliminary request for information is **not** a substitute for these rights.

During the process, an employee may explain the circumstances, identify factual mistakes, provide relevant material, request that suitable witnesses or evidence be considered, and ask HR about reasonable procedural accommodations or extensions. An employee must not be pressured to confess or threatened for offering a legitimate response.

An allegation or temporary security restriction does **not** mean the employee has been found guilty.

## Temporary Protective Measures

In a credible safety, confidentiality, harassment or security situation, Blinto may take necessary protective steps such as temporarily limiting access, separating the people involved, or changing reporting arrangements while facts are reviewed.

Any **suspension pending inquiry** must follow applicable employment law, including requirements for written notice, duration and payments. Protective measures should be reviewed regularly and must not be presented as a final disciplinary finding.

## Outcome, Review and Respectful Treatment

HR communicates the appropriate outcome to the affected employee. Formal disciplinary decisions must be communicated in writing, including sufficient information about the decision and any applicable review or grievance route.

Possible outcomes may include an allegation not being substantiated, coaching or other appropriate nonpunitive measures, corrective action under the existing policy, or a legally compliant disciplinary decision where misconduct is established.

Employees may ask HR to review a factual mistake or procedural concern. A person accused of wrongdoing, a complainant and any witnesses should be treated respectfully, without retaliation or unnecessary publicity.

**No automatic salary deduction, arbitrary financial penalty or punishment based on an unverified allegation is authorized by this SOP.**

# Part B — Internal HR and Management Procedure

## 1. Receive and Triage the Report

People Operations/HR receives the report, records its general nature and date, identifies who may be involved, and preserves the minimum relevant information needed for review. HR decides whether the matter appears to call for ordinary coaching, a grievance response, initial fact-finding, or a formal misconduct inquiry.

Where a complaint involves harassment or discrimination, coordinate with the relevant grievance policy and any legally required complaints mechanism rather than replacing it with an informal disciplinary interview.

The Department Head supplies operational context but does not independently determine guilt or authorize a major disciplinary penalty.

## 2. Protect People, Systems and Relevant Evidence

Where reasonably necessary, HR and the appropriate system owner coordinate immediate protective steps, including preserving work records, temporarily restricting at-risk access, or addressing contact between involved employees.

A security measure must use the existing [Access Provisioning & Deprovisioning](./access-provisioning-and-deprovisioning.md) and [Credential & Password Management Policy](./credential-and-password-management-policy.md) processes. Do not circulate passwords or sensitive employee evidence through ordinary task comments or group chats.

Consider conflicts of interest before selecting a reviewer or investigator. A complainant, subject of the allegation, key witness, or person with a material personal interest must not control findings about their own case.

## 3. Conduct Preliminary Fact-Finding When Appropriate

HR may speak privately with relevant individuals, examine available communications or documents lawfully accessible for the purpose, and check whether there is a credible basis to proceed. Gather information supporting **and** contradicting the initial allegation.

A short internal turnaround target or a request for a preliminary explanation may help HR decide what to do next, but **no such target shortens the statutory reply period or inquiry protections** for a formal section 23–24 proceeding. An employee's preliminary explanation must not be treated as a waiver of formal rights.

If the allegation lacks a sufficient basis, HR should close or redirect it without creating an unsupported disciplinary finding. Where the issue is ordinary performance or correctable conduct, use the established corrective-action policy instead.

## 4. Determine Legal Coverage and Formal Process

Before commencing formal discipline, HR reviews the person's actual duties, legal classification, engagement terms, applicable approved service rules, and the law currently in force. **Job title alone does not determine whether a person is a statutory “worker.”** Seek Bangladesh employment-law advice when applicability or procedure is uncertain.

For a covered worker and a contemplated punishment under **section 23**, follow **section 24 of the Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, as amended**. In particular:

- State the allegations **in writing** and give the worker a copy.
- Allow the worker **at least seven days** to submit an explanation; do not substitute a shorter informal or preliminary period.
- Give the worker a meaningful **opportunity to be heard**.
- Arrange the legally required inquiry by a committee with **equal numbers of employer and worker representatives**; complete the inquiry within the applicable **60-day** statutory period.
- Permit the worker to receive assistance from a person employed at the establishment whom the worker nominates, as provided by the Act.
- Where oral witnesses are presented, respect the worker's legally provided opportunity to examine witnesses against them.
- Obtain approval of any applicable punishment order from the legally required employer or manager as well as Blinto's internal authorized decision-maker.
- Give the worker a copy of the punishment order and retain the necessary record.

If another mandatory procedure applies (including an applicable service rule or specific workplace-harassment process), HR must comply with that procedure as well. This SOP does not presume every investigation is automatically a section 24 proceeding.

## 5. Manage Any Suspension Lawfully

Suspension pending a statutory misconduct inquiry is not the same as a temporary restriction of system access. For a covered worker, section 24 requires a **written suspension order** and generally limits suspension pending inquiry to **60 days** unless the case is pending before a court, with statutory **subsistence allowance and other applicable allowances** payable during suspension.

If misconduct is not proved, section 24 provides for treatment of the suspension period as service and payment of wages for the period after adjustment of subsistence allowance. Other outcomes and payments must follow the Act. HR and Finance must verify the correct legal basis, allowance calculation and treatment; managers must not casually impose unpaid investigatory leave.

Where the employee is not governed by those statutory provisions, any temporary leave or suspension must still be justified under applicable law and contract and authorized appropriately.

## 6. Examine Evidence and Hear the Employee

An impartial investigator or legally constituted inquiry committee considers the allegation, relevant records, the employee's explanation, witnesses where appropriate, and material mitigating or exculpatory information.

Do not base a finding solely on rumor, unsupported assumptions, or one-sided statements when reasonably available evidence points elsewhere. Give the employee an opportunity to respond to material concerns affecting the decision, subject to lawful confidentiality and witness-protection safeguards.

Keep sensitive details in a restricted, appropriate HR record, not in public ClickUp tasks or general Telegram groups. Record the reasoning behind findings, including where evidence is insufficient or contradictory.

## 7. Review the Proposed Outcome and Approval Authority

HR assesses the outcome and advises on proportionality, relevant employee history, seriousness, mitigating circumstances and legal requirements. The [Corrective Action & Performance Improvement Policy](./corrective-action-and-performance-improvement-policy.md) governs suitable nonstatutory coaching or improvement measures; **formal statutory penalties must follow their own legal prerequisites**.

**Internal authority selected for this draft:** The **Chief of Staff** reviews serious disciplinary matters and provides final internal approval for disciplinary outcomes **other than involuntary termination or dismissal**, subject to any legally required employer/manager authorization.

**Approval reconciliation:** The currently approved [Employee Offboarding](./employee-offboarding.md) SOP specifies **Team/Department Lead recommendation → HR review → CEO final approval** for involuntary termination. That approved authority must remain in effect for a dismissal or other involuntary termination until formally amended. Chief of Staff review does not, by itself, replace CEO final approval. Management may later update both documents consistently through an authorized change.

HR should obtain legal advice where proceedings, proposed penalties, statutory classification, worker representation, suspension or dismissal rights are uncertain.

## 8. Communicate the Decision and Allow Review

HR conveys the relevant result to the employee respectfully and, for formal disciplinary decisions, **in writing**. Explain the decision and any immediate obligations or applicable mechanism to raise a procedural or factual concern.

The employee's explanation and any substantiated correction should be considered and retained as appropriate. An appeal or review request does not create a right to interfere with another person's confidential records, but a genuine procedural error should be investigated and corrected.

Communicate only necessary information to the reporting person, witnesses, Department Head and system owners. Avoid sharing protected employment details more widely than necessary.

## 9. Close, Secure and Follow Up

HR maintains the report, evidence, notice, employee response, inquiry records, approval and decision in a controlled location under applicable privacy and retention requirements. Records must be factual, access-restricted and preserved appropriately for legitimate employment or legal purposes.

Where the outcome requires coaching, a warning, a PIP, access changes or separation, hand the implementation to the **canonical SOP that owns that activity**. For separation, use the approved Employee Offboarding process and any applicable final settlement policy.

Review whether protective measures remain necessary. Close unfounded allegations without an unsupported misconduct notation. Consider proportionate steps to address a substantiated workplace risk, including protection against retaliation.

## Accountability

**Employees and complainants:** Report concerns honestly, cooperate in good faith, provide relevant information and avoid retaliation or circulation of allegations.

**People Operations / HR:** Coordinate triage, procedural fairness, confidentiality, lawful notices, records and communication; seek legal review where needed.

**Department Head:** Supply relevant work context, preserve appropriate records, refer serious concerns to HR and avoid prejudging disputed allegations.

**Uninvolved investigator / inquiry committee:** Review evidence impartially and follow any mandatory statutory composition and hearing requirements.

**Chief of Staff:** Review serious matters and provide final internal approval for nontermination disciplinary decisions within legal limits.

**CEO:** Retain final approval for involuntary termination under the currently approved Employee Offboarding SOP until a formally approved change is made.

**Finance / system owner:** Implement only lawful authorized pay or access actions through the applicable processes.

## Current Practice and Proposed Improvements

Blinto currently has **no standalone disciplinary investigation SOP**. Employees report uncertainty about how to raise allegations; investigation responsibilities, evidence documentation, confidentiality and written decisions need clearer standards.

This draft **proposes** a defined HR intake route, independent review where necessary, controlled case documentation, formal written decisions, and a clear employee-response process. It does **not** claim Blinto already operates an electronic case-management system, confidential hotline, anonymous reporting portal, specialized investigation unit or new HRMS workflow.

## Legal Basis and Pre-Approval Review

The core statutory safeguards summarized here come from the **Bangladesh Labour Act, 2006, sections 23 and 24, as amended**, together with applicable Labour Rules and binding service rules. Section 24 addresses written charges, at least seven days for explanation, hearing and committee inquiry, statutory inquiry time limits, pending-inquiry suspension, employee assistance, witness examination and written penalty orders.

The Labour Act was amended again in **April 2026**. HR and Bangladesh employment counsel should verify the current consolidated legislation, statutory worker coverage, applicable service rules and any case-specific procedures before formally adopting this SOP or using it for a dismissal.
